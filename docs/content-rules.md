# STL Home Journey content rules

These are George Kindler's standing rules for every page on stlhomejourney.com. Apply them to new pages and to every page you rebuild.

## 1. Page structure: one topic, question subtopics, reader flow

Pages are built to be quoted by AI tools and still read well top to bottom.

- **One page, one topic.** The topic is the main thing a person is trying to figure out, such as "When should I lower my price?" or "Buying in Oakville."
- **Subtopics are the questions people actually ask.** Each one is a heading. Write it the way a person would type it.
- **Answer first.** Open with a sentence or two that can be quoted on its own. Then give the proof: a 2026 number, a table or a rule. Then George's take, in his voice.
- **Order the questions for the reader.** Follow the path a reader takes through the topic, for example the total, then each cost, then what you control. Don't use the order the questions were written in.
- **Top of the page: 3 to 5 headline questions** that sum up the page. Don't put a wall of questions there.
- **Make section headings questions where it reads naturally.** The answer sits under its own heading once. Don't give it as a quick answer and then repeat it as a section.
- **Related questions sit as subheadings (h3) under the section they belong to.**
- **Bottom of the page: only the edge cases** that don't fit a section.
- **Stories and data keep plain headings.** George's case studies, such as the Imperial listing, and data tables don't get forced into questions.
- **Each question lives on one page.** Anywhere else it comes up, give one line and a link to the page that owns it. This prevents cannibalization.
- **Only add questions you can answer with evidence or George's own experience.** No padding. As a guide, focused articles carry about 12 to 18 questions, and community pages and hubs about 20 to 35.
- **FAQ schema matches what's visible.** Every question in FAQPage JSON-LD appears on the page, in reading order. No schema-only questions.

## 2. Data and accuracy

- Use only real data: 2026 closed sales, published sources or George's stated experience. Don't invent facts.
- No conflicting numbers anywhere on the site. When a figure appears on more than one page, it matches the page that owns it.
- Cite outside facts by name, for example "NAR's 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers" or "Missouri DESE 2024–2025 APR."
- Label past sales as past sales, not current inventory.
- No "working to verify," "not confirmed" or "could not be verified" text on public pages.
- Fix typos and repeated sentences whenever you find them.

## 3. House style ("better safe than sorry")

- Don't make school-quality or reputation claims, and don't rank or editorialize about schools. Cited facts, such as DESE APR scores, are fine when stated plainly.
- Don't use "family/families" to describe neighborhoods.
- Don't steer buyers toward or away from areas, and don't disparage any area.
- Write "Mehlville R-IX" in full. Webster Groves is not South County.
- Don't cut content from pages that AI tools are already citing. Add to them instead.

## 4. George's positions (use his words)

- **Concessions:** never use one to generate a contract. Concessions belong in repair negotiations after the inspection, where they typically run $2,000 to $8,000. Homes that sell over asking rarely give one unless there's a major repair.
- **Price reduction vs. concession or agent bonus:** lower the price. Buyers move first on price, then on inspections.
- **Property taxes:** listing taxes are the seller's most recent bill. Use them to compare areas, then confirm the exact address with the St. Louis County Collector of Revenue.
- **Earnest money:** about 1% is typical for a retail sale with a financed buyer. On cash deals George has always recommended 10%, and sellers taking an off-market offer should ask for more.
- **Cash offers:** in George's experience, wholesale cash offers land around 50% of market value, rarely more than 60 to 65 cents on the dollar.
