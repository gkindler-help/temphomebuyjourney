Questions This Tool Answers

How much house can a doctor afford in St. Louis?

It depends on the rule your advisor uses, not the lender’s maximum. Under the common 2× income rule, a household earning $300,000 can borrow about $600,000, about a $631,000 price with 5% down. A 50/30/20 budget with $16,500 in monthly take-home pay, $2,500 in other needs and a $1,500 student loan payment lands closer to $522,000. A new attending who keeps spending near resident levels for a few years buys less. If you own your practice or take 1099 pay, start from what your tax returns show, not your gross. How do I calculate a physician mortgage payment?

Add principal and interest on the loan to property tax, insurance and upkeep. On a $631,579 house with 5% down at 6.75%, principal and interest is about $3,892 a month; with property taxes near the hospitals, insurance and upkeep, the total is about $5,143.

How much does a physician loan save versus 20% down?

It doesn’t make the house cheaper; it keeps cash invested. On a $631,579 house, putting 5% down instead of 20% leaves about $94,700 in your portfolio, in exchange for a bigger loan and sometimes a slightly higher rate.

What does the 2× income rule mean for a physician’s mortgage?

Keep the mortgage at or below twice your gross household income. At $300,000, that’s a $600,000 loan, which supports about a $631,000 price with 5% down. It’s a guideline many physician-focused planners use, not a lender limit.

How does a 50/30/20 budget work for a physician’s house?

Half of take-home pay covers needs, including housing and minimum loan payments; 30% goes to saving and investing; 20% to wants or extra debt payments. Housing gets whatever is left of the 50% after your other needs and student loan payment.

Should I pay off student loans or buy a house first?

It depends on your loan rate, any forgiveness you qualify for and how long you’ll stay, so settle it with your advisor. Most physician financial plans build a cash reserve and clear high-interest debt first. The tool lets you pick: invest while paying the minimums, split extra cash 50/50, or pay the loans off and rent until they’re gone.

How the numbers work

  • Prices and taxes are 2026 medians from single-family sales inside each city, from this site’s city guides. Your house will differ.
  • Monthly cost is principal and interest on a 30-year loan, plus the city’s median tax rate, 0.4% of the price a year for insurance and 1% for upkeep.
  • Cash for the house is what you’ve saved, minus the reserve and any high-interest debt you clear first, the order most physician financial plans follow.
  • The 50/30/20 budget gives 50% of take-home pay to needs, including housing and minimum loan payments, 30% to saving and investing and 20% to wants or extra debt payments.
  • Cash to close is the down payment plus 2.5% closing costs. Selling is counted at 9.5%, the middle of the 8% to 11% St. Louis range.
  • Growth rates are assumptions you control. St. Louis County price per square foot rose about 5% a year from 2021 to 2025; that’s history, not a forecast.
  • This is real estate math, not investment, tax or legal advice. Your advisor and lender should run your own numbers.
