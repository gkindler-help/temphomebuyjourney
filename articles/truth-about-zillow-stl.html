The Truth About Zillow

The Truth About Zillow in St. Louis: What Buyers and Sellers Need to Know

Zillow is the most visited real estate website in the country. Most St. Louis users have no idea how it makes money — or what happens to their information when they click a button. Here is the full picture.

Roughly 68% of homebuyers use Zillow during their search. Most sellers in St. Louis check their Zestimate before they list. Most of them have no idea how Zillow actually makes money — or what happens to their information when they engage with the platform.

This is not an anti-Zillow page. Zillow has useful tools. The search functionality works. The data is real. But Zillow is a business. It generates revenue by selling access to you — the buyer or seller — to agents and mortgage companies who pay for that access. Understanding that model changes how you use the platform.

Documented, Not Opinion In 2025, Zillow faced eight separate lawsuits from consumers, competitors, and federal regulators including the FTC, alleging deceptive practices and violations of federal consumer protection law. A University of Pennsylvania Wharton professor published a study in March 2026 finding that only 0.3% of Zillow users understood they were not contacting the listing agent when they clicked "Contact Agent." These are allegations in active federal litigation. Zillow denies wrongdoing.

Before You Trust What You See on Zillow

Zillow is where most St. Louis buyers start their search. It is not where you should make decisions. Two homes at the same listing price in St. Louis can have monthly payments that differ by $300 to $400 depending solely on their zip code. Zillow's search does not show you that difference. It shows you the listing price.
