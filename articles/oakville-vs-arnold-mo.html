Oakville vs. Arnold: What 2026 Sales Show

Last updated October 22, 2026

Oakville and Arnold sit on opposite sides of the Meramec River and the county line, and in 2026 that line showed up in two places: price and taxes. The Oakville High area’s median was $385,000 and Arnold’s $305,000. Arnold’s median property tax was $1,741, less than half of Oakville’s $3,720.

An Arnold mailing address doesn’t mean the house is inside Arnold city limits, and Oakville isn’t a city at all. Check the exact address, because it decides the rules that apply when you buy and sell.

Oakville vs. Arnold Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first, from 2026 closed single-family sales: 137 listed in MLS as Oakville High area (January 5 through October 5) and 97 with an Arnold mailing address (January 8 through October 1). Those are the same groups the Oakville and Arnold pages use. These are past sales, not what’s for sale today.

Is Oakville more expensive than Arnold?

Yes. The Oakville High area’s 2026 median was $385,000 and Arnold’s $305,000, an $80,000 gap. Comparing one-story ranches of similar size, Oakville ran higher too: ranches of 1,200–1,499 sq ft sold for a median $306,000 in Oakville and $255,000 in Arnold.

Are property taxes lower in Arnold than in Oakville?

Yes, by about half. The median annual property tax on 2026 sales was $1,741 in Arnold and $3,720 in the Oakville High area, according to MLS sales records. That’s about $2,000 a year, or roughly $165 a month in a mortgage payment. As a share of price it was about 0.64% in Arnold and 0.99% in Oakville.

What does $300,000 buy in Oakville vs. Arnold?

Far more chances in Arnold. At $300,000 or less, a 2026 budget reached 47 of 97 Arnold sales (48.5%) and 24 of 137 Oakville High-area sales (17.5%). At that budget the Arnold homes were built at a median 1966 and the Oakville homes in 1963.

What about $350,000 or $400,000?

At $350,000 or less: 68 of 97 Arnold sales (70.1%) and 53 of 137 in Oakville (38.7%). At $400,000 or less: 74 (76.3%) and 79 (57.7%). By $400,000, Oakville had more homes in reach in total, but a smaller share of its market.

Do homes sell faster in Oakville or Arnold?

About equally fast. The median days on market (CDOM) was 7 in Oakville and 8 in Arnold. 79 of 137 Oakville sales (57.7%) and 51 of 97 Arnold sales (52.6%) closed above the original list price. The median Oakville sale closed at 101.2% of the original list price; Arnold’s at 100.0%.

Which has newer homes, Oakville or Arnold?

Arnold, slightly. Arnold’s median 2026 sale was built in 1987 and Oakville’s in 1979. 22 of 97 Arnold sales (22.7%) were built in 2000 or later, versus 24 of 137 in Oakville (17.5%).

Property Taxes: The Biggest Difference

Across 2026 sales, the median annual property tax was $1,741 with an Arnold mailing address and $3,720 in the Oakville High area. On a typical house that’s about $2,000 a year, which changes what a monthly payment buys. The tax on a listing is the seller’s most recent bill, so confirm the exact address with the county collector before you make an offer: the St. Louis County Collector of Revenue for Oakville and the Jefferson County Collector for Arnold.

School Districts and Local Rules

All 97 Arnold-address sales in 2026 were in the Fox C-6 School District; the Oakville High area is in Mehlville R-IX. Confirm the district for the exact address. The rules at sale differ too. Unincorporated St. Louis County, which includes Oakville, requires an occupancy permit when a new occupant moves in. Inside Arnold city limits, the city requires a property maintenance inspection; Arnold-address homes outside the city fall under Jefferson County, which has no resale inspection of its own.
