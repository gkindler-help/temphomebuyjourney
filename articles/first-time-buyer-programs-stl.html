St. Louis Down Payment Guide

First-Time Home Buyer Programs in St. Louis

Last updated October 13, 2026

Most St. Louis first-time buyers have more help available than they think: a state program through MHDC, local assistance in the City or County, and federal loans that need little or nothing down. Here’s who qualifies for each, which to check first, and what the money really is.

George Kindler·Licensed Missouri Realtor·For Buyers

First-Time Buyer Programs, Answered

What first-time home buyer programs can I use in St. Louis?

The primary down payment program is MHDC’s First Place and Next Step, available anywhere in the St. Louis area. Beyond that, the City of St. Louis and some County partners run smaller local programs, and federal FHA, VA and USDA loans need 3.5% down or less.

Who counts as a first-time home buyer?

Usually anyone who hasn’t owned their primary residence in the past three years, not just people who have never owned. MHDC and the City’s HomeSTL program both use that three-year rule. If you owned a home years ago and have rented since, you likely qualify.

Which program should I check first?

MHDC. Start with an MHDC-certified lender, because MHDC is the main down payment program in the St. Louis area, works anywhere in the region and sits on top of FHA, VA, USDA and conventional loans. Local City or County programs come second, if you qualify and funds are open.

Is down payment assistance free money?

Usually not. Most assistance is a second loan that’s forgiven only if you stay in the home long enough. MHDC’s 4% assistance, for example, is forgiven between years five and ten. Selling or refinancing early can mean paying some of it back.

What does MHDC offer first-time buyers?

MHDC is the primary down payment assistance program for St. Louis buyers, so it’s where I’d focus first. It offers two mortgage programs, each with an optional assistance loan of 4% of your first mortgage for down payment and closing costs. The Missouri Housing Development Commission runs them through certified lenders, not directly, so you ask for them by name when you get pre-approved.

  • First Place is for first-time buyers, qualified veterans and buyers in federally targeted census tracts.
  • Next Step is open to first-time and repeat buyers, with higher income and price limits.

For 2026 in the St. Louis area, regular First Place income limits start at $113,500 for a household of one or two, and the one-family price cap is $566,354. MHDC’s materials list a 640 minimum credit score, and it works on top of an FHA, VA, USDA or conventional loan (compare those loans). The full limits, fees, seller paperwork and how much of the 4% you actually keep are in the MHDC First Place and Next Step guide.

What if I’ve owned a home before?

Look at Next Step. It doesn’t require you to be a first-time buyer. First Place also waives the first-time rule for qualified veterans and for homes in targeted census tracts, so check the exact address with your lender.

What help does the City of St. Louis offer?

The City’s main program is HomeSTL, run by the St. Louis Development Corporation. When it reopened in May 2026, it offered a 0% forgivable loan of up to $40,000 toward a home in the City, or up to $50,000 in a HUD-designated census tract, for first-time buyers under 80% of the area median income. It requires a homebuyer education course and an SLDC-approved lender, and the 2026 round was a limited fund paid first come, first served.

Subject to change Local program amounts, eligibility and availability change with each year’s funding. Confirm current terms and whether funds are open directly with SLDC’s HomeSTL program before you count on it.

Can I buy a house from the City’s land bank?

Yes. The City’s Land Reutilization Authority sells properties it owns, usually vacant and in need of major work, often below market and with a requirement to rehab them. It’s a renovation project, not a turnkey first home, so read what buyers regret about cheap houses before you bid.

What help is there in St. Louis County?

Mainly through nonprofit and municipal partners rather than one County program. Beyond Housing’s down payment assistance program serves St. Louis County, Jefferson County, St. Charles County, O’Fallon and Florissant, as a 0% loan forgiven after five years of living in the home. Some municipalities also run their own programs with federal HOME funds, so ask the city you’re buying in.

Subject to change Amounts, income and asset limits, and availability are set by each year’s funding. Confirm current terms directly with Beyond Housing or the municipality before you shop.

Which federal loans help first-time buyers?

Three loans and one discount program:

  • FHA: 3.5% down with a 580 or higher credit score, under HUD’s rules. Mortgage insurance is required.
  • VA: no down payment for eligible veterans and service members, and no monthly mortgage insurance. See what kills VA deals in St. Louis.
  • USDA: no down payment in eligible areas, which include parts of Jefferson, St. Charles and outer St. Louis County. Check the exact address on USDA’s eligibility map.
  • Good Neighbor Next Door: HUD sells some of its own homes in revitalization areas at 50% off list price to teachers, law enforcement officers, firefighters and EMTs who agree to live there for three years. It only applies when HUD has homes in those areas.

How much each one needs down is on the down payment page.

Can first-time buyer assistance run out?

Local programs can. HomeSTL’s 2026 round was a fixed fund paid first come, first served, and County partner programs depend on each year’s federal funding. That’s one more reason to build your plan around MHDC and treat local help as a bonus. Ask about availability when you start your pre-approval, not after you’re under contract.

How do I apply for first-time buyer assistance?

Through your lender, before you start touring seriously. In order:

  1. Get pre-approved with an MHDC-certified lender and ask about First Place and Next Step by name. MHDC lists its certified lenders on mhdc.com.
  2. Then check local help. In the City, ask whether your lender is SLDC-approved for HomeSTL. In the County, contact Beyond Housing or the city you’re buying in. Do it before you’re under contract; eligibility checks take time.
  3. VA or USDA? Confirm eligibility first: a VA Certificate of Eligibility, or the property’s USDA eligibility.
  4. Tell your agent which program you’re using before you write the offer. Some programs involve the seller’s paperwork too.

These programs cover one piece of the purchase: the money. For the rest, from search to closing, use the complete guide to buying a home in St. Louis.

More First-Time Buyer Questions

Can I use more than one assistance program?

Sometimes. Each program sets its own rules on combining, and your lender has to approve the layering. Ask your lender to show every program you qualify for side by side, with the second-loan terms of each.
