When Can I Move In After Closing in St. Louis?

Last updated October 21, 2026

The keys come at closing, but the move-in date depends on more than the closing date: what the contract says about possession, whether the seller needs to stay, and what your city or the County requires before anyone moves in.

George Kindler· Licensed Missouri Realtor· Buyers

Moving In After Closing, Answered

When do I get the keys to a house in St. Louis?

At closing. The St. Louis contract calls for possession at closing unless a rider changes it, so the house is yours once the title company confirms the sale is complete. Before that, it’s still the seller’s. How possession works ↓

Can I move in before closing?

Only with a possession-prior-to-closing rider in the contract, and I typically don’t recommend it unless it’s a special circumstance. If the financing falls through after you’ve moved in, you’re living in a house you don’t own, and the sellers are out of their home without their proceeds. Why I avoid it ↓

Can the seller stay in the house after closing?

Only if the contract says so, usually as a rent-back. Possession after closing typically means a seller holding over, and I don’t like them. If you agree to one, put the rent, the move-out date and who pays for damage in writing before you close. The terms to insist on ↓

Can I move in the day I close?

Usually, but check the address first. In unincorporated St. Louis County, a new occupant has to wait until the County issues the occupancy permit, and some cities require their own inspection at every sale. Occupancy rules by area ↓

What should I check at the final walk-through?

That the house is in the condition you bought it, that agreed repairs are done with paperwork to prove it, and that fixtures are still there. If something’s wrong, raise it before closing, when the seller can still fix it. The walk-through checklist ↓

Why does possession happen at closing?

Because that’s when ownership changes. The contract calls for possession at closing unless the parties add a rider that says otherwise. Until the title company confirms the sale is complete, the seller still owns the house, carries the insurance and is responsible for its condition. After that, it’s yours. What happens on closing day →

Why don’t I recommend moving in before closing?

Because if the financing falls through after the buyer moves in, everyone is in trouble. A possession-prior-to-closing rider lets a buyer move in early, but until closing there are too many unknowns:

  • When will the financing go through, and will it go through at all?
  • Will the buyer move back out if it doesn’t?
  • Can the sellers move back in? They’re out of their home without their sale proceeds.
  • Who has the utilities? They have to be moved back to the right party.

It can be a mess. My philosophy is to keep it straightforward: possession at closing, unless it’s a special circumstance.

What happens when the seller needs to stay after closing?

You become their landlord for a while, so treat it like one. Possession after closing is typically a seller holding over, and I don’t like them. I’ve had a few, and the way to make one work is to settle everything in writing before you close:

  • Rent and the move-out date. The buyer is taking on risk, and the seller pays for it in price, concessions or rent.
  • The walk-through and damages stay true to the original contract. There’s no second walk-through after the rental period to reopen a sale that already closed.
  • Move-out falls under the rental period. Define in writing what the seller is responsible for when they leave.
  • Once it closes, it isn’t renegotiated.

Sellers should know the other side of this too: I’ve seen a required rent-back keep offers from coming in at all. Once it was removed, the house was under contract within a weekend. Rent-backs from the seller’s side →

Do I need an occupancy permit before I move in?

It depends on the address, not the ZIP code. Some cities and the County require their own inspection or permit when a house changes hands, and it’s separate from your home inspection.

What does a good final walk-through cover?

That you’re getting the house you agreed to buy. Walk it the way you saw it at the inspection, and check three things:

  • Agreed repairs. I ask for written documentation of any repair the seller agreed to before the walk-through, not at the closing table. Paperwork is what settles a dispute: on a 2024 Maplewood sale, the buyer’s returning inspector marked 26 of 30 items not resolved, and the contractor’s bills didn’t say which work was finished. What a failed walk-through cost that seller →
  • Condition. Nothing new damaged in the move-out, and systems still working.
  • Fixtures. Anything attached to the house that wasn’t excluded in the contract stays with it. Removing one before closing is a material change in condition, and the buyer can have the seller cure it before closing.

More Questions About Moving In

Rent-backs

Do I get another walk-through when a rent-back ends?

Not one that reopens the sale. The original contract’s walk-through and damage terms stand, and what the seller owes at move-out belongs in the written rent-back terms. Agree on that before closing, because it won’t be renegotiated after. Rent-back terms ↑

What if I have to sell my house before I can move?

Then the two closings have to line up, or you plan a short-term rental or a rent-back on your own sale. It depends on your financial position and whether you can carry two payments. Selling and buying at the same time →
