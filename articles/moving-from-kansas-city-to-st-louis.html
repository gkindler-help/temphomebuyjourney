Moving From Kansas City to St. Louis: Find the Area With the House You’re Leaving

Last updated October 30, 2026

A Kansas City, Missouri move to St. Louis keeps a lot the same: the state taxes, the assessment rules, even the basements. What changes is the map. This page matches the kind of house you’re leaving on the Missouri side of Kansas City to the St. Louis areas built in the same era, from 2026 sales.

Moving From Kansas City, Answered

What part of St. Louis is most like Kansas City’s suburbs?

Match the era. Lee’s Summit’s 1990s houses point to Wildwood, Raytown and Independence ranches to Crestwood, Concord and Mehlville, Hyde Park and Westport to South City, and Brookside to Kirkwood and Webster Groves. The full match ↓

Where should I look in St. Louis if I’m leaving Lee’s Summit?

Wildwood for the same 1990s houses, Imperial for a lower price. Lee’s Summit’s median house dates from 1995; Wildwood’s 2026 sales had a 1992 median, and Imperial’s a 1999 median at $354,500. Newer-suburb matches ↓

Where are ranch houses in St. Louis like Raytown and Independence?

In South County. Raytown’s median house dates from 1964 and Independence’s from 1967; Crestwood’s 2026 sales had a 1957 median with 72.5% ranches, and Concord and Mehlville both had 1966 medians. Ranch matches ↓

What stays the same moving from Kansas City to St. Louis?

Your state taxes, the 19% residential assessment and the basements. If you move from Kansas City, Missouri into St. Louis City, you keep a 1% earnings tax; move into St. Louis County and you drop it. Same state, new map ↓

How far is St. Louis from Kansas City?

About 250 miles on I-70, roughly three and a half to four hours by car. That’s close enough to house-hunt on weekends instead of buying sight unseen. What that makes easier ↓

What kind of Kansas City-area house are you leaving?

Kansas City, Missouri is older than its reputation: its median house dates from 1969, and 21.3% of its housing predates 1940, according to Census data. The newer houses sit in Lee’s Summit and the Northland. St. Louis has the same spread, just in different places.

Leaving Lee’s Summit, the Northland or Blue Springs?

Start with Wildwood for age, Imperial for price. Lee’s Summit put up 41.8% of its housing in the 1990s and 2000s, for a 1995 median; Blue Springs’ median is 1988 and Liberty’s 1984, according to Census data.

Leaving a Raytown or Independence ranch?

South County is built from the same decades. Raytown’s median house dates from 1964 and Independence’s from 1967, with about half of Independence’s housing built from the 1950s through the 1970s, according to Census data.

Leaving a shirtwaist or bungalow in Hyde Park or Westport?

Go to South City, and match on age and brick rather than style. The Kansas City shirtwaist, a brick or stone first floor under a frame upper story, is a local form without an exact St. Louis twin. What St. Louis has instead is block after block of 1910s–40s brick bungalows and flats in Shaw, Southwest Garden and Tower Grove South.

Just outside the City, Maplewood’s 2026 median house was built in 1929, at $295,000. Like Hyde Park, these are walkable streets where the house itself is the draw. South City neighborhoods →

Leaving Brookside or the Country Club District?

Kirkwood and Webster Groves are the closest in feel: established, tree-lined, with walkable downtowns and houses from the 1930s to 1950s. Their 2026 medians were $566,750 in Kirkwood and $444,500 in Webster Groves. For Mission Hills or the top of Ward Parkway, look at Clayton, at $1,240,000, and Ladue, at $1,430,000.

If you’re leaving a downtown or Crossroads loft instead, look at Lafayette Square, Soulard and the Central West End. Mid-County compared →

What stays the same on a Kansas City to St. Louis move, and what changes?

More stays the same than on any out-of-state move.

  • Same state rules. Same Missouri income tax, same 19% residential assessment with reassessment in odd-numbered years, and no transfer tax on either end.
  • Same earnings-tax logic. Kansas City, Missouri and St. Louis City each charge a 1% earnings tax. Move into the City and you keep it; move into St. Louis County and it goes away, unless you work in the City. The earnings tax →
  • Same basements and clay. You already know to check the walls; add a sewer scope, $150 to $300, which I recommend on every St. Louis house.

What changes is the map. Kansas City, Missouri spreads across four counties; St. Louis City sits in no county at all, separate from St. Louis County and its 88 municipalities, and many of those cities require an occupancy inspection when a house sells.
