How Much House Can You Afford Making $100K in St. Louis?

Last updated October 2, 2026

On a $100,000 salary in St. Louis, a comfortable budget is about $283K, and most lenders will approve up to about $368K with typical debts. At this income the question changes from "what can I get approved for" to "which trade-offs do I want": St. Charles County's lower taxes and newer homes, or South County and Fenton closer to the city. Here's the math and how the choices compare.

The Math: $100K Salary → $283K–$368K Home

Lenders start from your gross monthly income — $8,333 on $100,000 — and apply two limits: what your housing payment can be, and what all of your debts together can be. The low end of the range keeps your full payment at 28% of gross income. The high end is what most lenders would approve at a 43% total debt-to-income ratio, assuming about $550 a month in other debts (car, student loans, credit cards).

$100K: Conventional Loans and the St. Charles Tax Advantage

Most $100K buyers use a conventional loan. With 5% down you'll pay private mortgage insurance (PMI), but unlike FHA it can be removed once you reach 20% equity — by request at 80% loan-to-value, and automatically at 78% under federal law. With 10% down or more, PMI drops further. Most down-payment assistance programs are out of reach at this income, so savings and loan pricing matter more.

The biggest monthly difference between your options is property tax. St. Charles County's 0.92% rate vs. 1.12% in South County is about $54 a month on a $325,000 house — more than $640 a year, every year.

The trade-off is distance. St. Peters, O'Fallon and Wentzville put more space and newer construction in your budget, but for jobs in Clayton or downtown, the I-70 or I-64 commute is a real cost in time and fuel.

Example: $100K Household With Childcare

Illustrative example, not a client story. Two earners make $100,000 combined, have a $550 car payment, and spend $1,200 a month on daycare.

  • Lender's view: approval up to about $368K. Daycare doesn't count as debt.
  • Real budget: with $1,200 of daycare, the comfortable number — about $283K — is the one to plan around, at least until daycare ends.
  • Payment at $325K: about $2,680 a month with 5% down, including PMI.
  • Cash to close at $325K: about $26,000 with 5% down, or $42,250 with 10% down.

Mistakes $100K Buyers Make

Ignoring the tax gap. The same $325K costs about $54 a month more in South County than in St. Charles County. Factor it into your comparison.

Not planning to remove PMI. Mark the date you'll reach 20% equity and ask your lender to remove PMI then — it won't always happen on its own until 22%.

Buying to the lender's limit because "we make six figures." At $368K, the payment is about 36% of gross income before daycare, retirement savings or college savings.

Underestimating the commute. An extra 30 minutes each way is about 250 hours a year.

$100K Affordability Questions

How much house can I afford on $100K in St. Louis?

About $283K comfortably and up to about $368K with typical debts, using a conventional loan with 5% down at the rate assumed on this page.

Is St. Charles County cheaper than South County?

On property taxes, yes: 0.92% vs. about 1.12%, or roughly $54 a month on a $325K home. Median prices in St. Peters and O'Fallon are similar to South County's 63129.

Do I need 20% down on a $100K salary?

No. Conventional loans allow 3–5% down with PMI, and PMI can be removed once you reach 20% equity.

How These Numbers Are Calculated

  • Interest rate: 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan. Your rate depends on credit, loan type and the market on the day you lock.
  • Property tax: 1.12% of the price for the budget range and payment table (common in St. Louis County); the area table shows each ZIP’s rate.
  • Insurance: about 1% of the home’s price per year. Missouri premiums vary widely — get quotes early.
  • Mortgage insurance: FHA 0.55% per year plus 1.75% upfront (financed); conventional PMI estimated at 0.60% with 5% down and 0.40% with 10% down.
  • Limits: comfortable = total housing payment at 28% of gross income; lender maximum = 43% total debt-to-income, minus the other debts stated.
  • Closing costs: estimated at 3% of the price.
  • Area data: median sale price, days on market and first-weekend sale rate from 7,006 residential sales, 2025–2026, MARIS MLS.
