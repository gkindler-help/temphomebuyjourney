St. Louis Home Repair Cost Guide

St. Louis Home Repair Cost Guide for Buyers: Know Before You Offer

Most buyers make an offer based on how a home feels. The items that cost $15,000 to $40,000 are invisible during a showing -- they are in the attic, under the slab, and behind the walls. Here is what to know before you write a number down.

George Kindler· Licensed Missouri Realtor· For Buyers

The Problem With How Most Buyers Evaluate Homes

You walk into a house. The kitchen was updated. The floors are refinished. George's eyes go to the ceiling in the basement, the age of the HVAC unit, the condition of the mortar between the bricks on the exterior. Most buyers' eyes go to the kitchen.

This is not a criticism -- it is how humans process spaces. But it means the most expensive problems in a St. Louis home are systematically overlooked during showings, and buyers routinely discover them after they are under contract, when the discovery costs them either renegotiation leverage or, if they are not careful, the repair itself.

St. Louis Is an Older Market A significant percentage of St. Louis housing stock was built before 1960. That means original clay tile sewer laterals, knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring in older homes, plaster walls, cast iron drain stacks, and tuckpointing that has not been touched in decades. These are not defects in most cases -- they are the reality of buying in this market. The question is whether the price reflects them.

The Big Ticket Items in St. Louis

Roof -- $8,000 to $20,000

A standard asphalt shingle roof on a St. Louis home runs $8,000 to $14,000 installed. Larger homes, steeper pitches, or tile and metal roofing run higher. At 20 to 25 years, a roof needs replacement regardless of how it looks from the street. Age and condition -- not appearance -- determines remaining life. Ask how old the roof is on every home you tour.

HVAC -- $5,000 to $15,000

A furnace replacement runs $3,000 to $6,000 installed. A central air condenser runs $3,500 to $6,000. A full system replacement with both units runs $7,000 to $15,000 depending on size and efficiency. At 15 years, HVAC equipment is in its final years of expected service life. At 20 years, it is on borrowed time. Factor this in before you offer.

Basement waterproofing -- $5,000 to $25,000

St. Louis has clay soil that expands when wet and puts significant lateral pressure on basement walls. Interior drainage systems with a sump pump run $5,000 to $12,000. Exterior excavation and waterproofing can run $15,000 to $25,000 or more. Look for efflorescence (white mineral deposits) on basement walls, staining at floor level, and any evidence of previous repairs.

Sewer lateral -- $4,000 to $12,000

Many St. Louis homes built before 1980 have original clay tile sewer laterals -- the pipe that runs from the house to the municipal sewer. These crack, offset, and collapse over time, and root intrusion is endemic. A camera inspection of the lateral costs $150 to $300 and is money well spent on any older St. Louis home. Replacement runs $4,000 to $12,000 depending on depth and length.

Tuckpointing -- $3,000 to $15,000

St. Louis has significant brick construction, and the mortar between bricks deteriorates over decades. Failed tuckpointing allows water intrusion that damages the brick, the structural integrity of the wall, and the interior finishes. Tuckpointing a full house runs $5,000 to $15,000. A single facade runs $1,500 to $4,000. Soft or missing mortar is visible during a careful exterior walkthrough.

Electrical -- $3,000 to $15,000

Homes built before 1960 may have knob-and-tube wiring that insurers will not cover without replacement. Homes from the 1960s and 70s may have aluminum branch circuit wiring that presents fire risk. A panel upgrade from 100 to 200 amp service runs $2,000 to $4,000. Full rewiring of an older home can run $8,000 to $15,000 or more.

How to Use This Information

During every showing, I evaluate the approximate remaining life of the major systems and flag visible deferred maintenance. That information goes into your offer strategy -- not as a reason to walk away, but as a basis for accurate pricing. A home that needs a new roof and HVAC in the near term is not worth the same as one with systems that were replaced two years ago, regardless of what the listing price says.
