How to Screen St. Louis Homes Before You Make a VA Offer

Last updated October 23, 2026

I don’t let my VA buyers write offers on houses that won’t pass the appraisal. I screen at the showing, before we write. Here’s the checklist I use, what to ask the listing agent first, and what to do when something turns up.

How do I screen a St. Louis house before a VA offer?

Walk it with the VA appraisal in mind, which takes about 15 minutes. Open the electrical panel, walk the outside for peeling paint on a pre-1978 house, check every set of stairs for a secure handrail, look at the roof from the ground, go to the basement, run the heat or air, look under sinks and check the windows.

What should I ask the listing agent before a VA showing?

What kind of electrical panel the house has, how old the roof is, whether there are known foundation problems or past repairs, whether the heating and air work, and whether the seller knows of any deferred maintenance. If the agent doesn’t know, check those things yourself at the showing.

What do I do if I find VA issues at the showing?

Write the repair into your offer with a contractor’s estimate attached, or move on to the next house. If the issue is expensive or the seller is unlikely to cooperate, moving on is usually the better call.

Should a VA buyer look at an as-is listing?

Usually not. An as-is listing tells you the seller won’t fix anything, and a VA appraisal requires certain repairs before closing.

Does screening replace a home inspection?

No. Screening catches what a VA appraiser is likely to condition. A home inspection, a radon test and a sewer scope cover the rest, and together they run about $600 to $1,050. I recommend all three on every St. Louis home, whatever its age.

The Showing Checklist

1. Electrical panel

Open the panel door. Look for Federal Pacific or FPE branding, “Stab-Lok” on the breakers, or breakers with orange or blue tips. Replacement runs about $2,000 to $4,000. If you find one, ask whether the seller will replace it before the appraisal. Federal Pacific panels →

2. Exterior paint on a pre-1978 house

Walk the outside. Check window and door frames, porch railings, siding and trim for peeling, chipping or flaking paint. On a house built before 1978, the VA requires that paint to be corrected.

3. Stairs and handrails

Check the front steps, the deck stairs and the basement stairway. A missing or loose handrail is a safety item an appraiser can condition. It’s a small fix to write into the offer.

4. Roof

Look up from the ground for missing shingles, sagging or obvious wear, and ask the roof’s age. The VA requires a roof that keeps moisture out and has reasonable life left. Replacement runs about $8,000 to $20,000.

5. Foundation and basement

Walk the basement walls and look at the floor. Hairline cracks are common; bowing walls, large cracks or active water are not. Minor foundation repairs run about $3,000 to $8,000 and major ones $15,000 to $40,000 or more.

6. Heating and air

Run the heat or the air and feel for airflow. The system has to work, not be new. A furnace alone runs about $3,000 to $6,000 and a full system $7,000 to $15,000.

7. Plumbing

Look under the sinks, around the toilets, near the water heater and at the basement ceiling under the bathrooms for active drips or water stains. A water heater replacement runs about $900 to $1,800.

8. Windows

Check for cracked or broken glass. It’s a minor fix, so write it into the offer.

Ask the Listing Agent First

  • What kind of electrical panel does the house have?
  • How old is the roof?
  • Any known foundation problems or past foundation repairs? If so, ask for the paperwork.
  • Do the heating and air work?
  • Does the seller know of any deferred maintenance?

If You Find Something

Either write the repair into your offer, with a contractor’s estimate attached so the seller knows exactly what they’re agreeing to, or move on. If the panel needs replacing and the seller won’t do it, the roof is shot and the seller won’t address it, or the foundation has major problems, I walk. There are other houses. More Screening a House for a VA Offer Questions

Can I skip screening and let the appraisal find the problems?

You can, but by then you’re under contract, the clock is running and you’ve paid for the appraisal and the inspection. Screening at the showing takes about 15 minutes.

Should I bring a flashlight to showings?

Yes. Basements are often poorly lit, and a flashlight helps with the panel, the foundation walls and the plumbing.
