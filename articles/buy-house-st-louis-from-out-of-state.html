Buying a St. Louis House From Out of State: How It Works When You Can’t Be Here

Last updated October 30, 2026

You can buy a St. Louis house without touring it in person. One of my buyers did it from Denver: he saw the house once, at an open house, and never walked it with me. The steps are the same as any purchase, but a few of them carry more risk when you’re not here, and those are the ones this page covers.

Buying From Out of State, Answered

How do you buy a house in St. Louis when you live out of state?

In five steps: get preapproved by a lender first, have your agent walk houses for you on live video, write the offer with the inspection contingency intact, use the inspection report to see what video can’t, then do the final walk-through on video and close remotely through the title company. What are the steps to buy a St. Louis house from out of state?

The same steps as any purchase, with your agent standing in for you at each one you can’t attend:

  1. Get preapproved first. A lender works by phone and email, and a seller won’t take an out-of-state offer seriously without proof you can close.
  2. Tour on live video, and keep a recording you can revisit: 360 photos or a private 3D tour.
  3. Write the offer from the comparable sales, with the inspection contingency intact.
  4. Inspect, and negotiate repairs from the report and its photos.
  5. Do the final walk-through on video and close remotely, with someone you trust picking up the keys.

A financed purchase usually closes 30 to 45 days after the contract, so if you have a move date, work backward from it.

What should your agent show you on a video tour?

The things that cost money, not the finishes you can see in the listing photos. On a live video walk-through, ask your agent to show you:

  • The furnace, air conditioner and water heater, with the labels, so you know their age.
  • The electrical panel, because some older panels, like Federal Pacific, can cause problems with insurance and with FHA and VA loans.
  • The basement walls and floor, where water and foundation problems show first.
  • The outside: the roof from the street, the grading, the gutters and the driveway.
  • The street and the neighbors’ houses, so you see what the listing photos crop out.

How it went on a real sale: I decode the furnace and air conditioner serial numbers at every showing, so my buyers know the age before the inspector does. For the Denver buyer, I walked the house on a live video call, brought my 360 camera and saved the photos to a shared folder he could go back through anytime. Going forward I’m using private 3D tours, which keep the whole walk-through in one place. The full walk-through checklist →

How do you write a strong offer when you can’t see the house again?

Price it from the comparable sales, not the listing’s story, and keep the protections you’d want if you were here. You won’t get a second showing before the inspection deadline, so the inspection contingency is the one to keep. A preapproval letter and a clear closing date make an out-of-state offer believable.

How it went on a real sale: Before the Denver buyer wrote, I pulled the comparable sales. The listing described the house as worth $180,000 and priced it at $160,000 as if it were a deal; the comps said it was worth $160,000, not $180,000. We went under contract at $160,000, and before any inspection the seller offered $30,000 off. Which contingencies to keep →

How do you handle the inspection from out of state?

Let the report and its photos be your eyes. You don’t have to be there; join by phone or video while the inspector goes over the findings, then read the full report. In St. Louis, add a sewer scope: much of the housing is decades old, and the sewer line from the house to the street belongs to the owner.

Then negotiate from the report like any buyer: repairs for the items that affect the loan, safety or moving in, and a credit or warranty for small things you can live with. How to use the inspection report →

How do you send earnest money and closing funds safely from out of state?

Verify every wire by phone, at a number you looked up yourself. Buying from a distance means more of the deal happens by email, and fake wiring instructions are the most expensive email you can get. Before you wire a single dollar, call your title company at a phone number you found on your own, not one from an email, and confirm the account and the amount.

How it went on a real sale: I watched an experienced investor lose $27,000 two days before closing to wiring instructions that looked exactly like the title company’s. That’s why I give every client the same rule on every deal. What I tell buyers before closing →

How do the final walk-through and closing work when you’re not here?

Your agent walks the house on video right before closing, so you see the condition yourself: that the agreed repairs were done and nothing changed. Then the title company handles the signing remotely. Ask them early how they do it, so the documents and the timing are set before closing week. Someone you trust can pick up the keys.
