Physicians Buying in St. Louis, Answered

Should a physician buy or rent in St. Louis?

Rent if you’ll be here less than about three years; buy if you expect to stay longer on a stable contract. Buying and later selling a St. Louis house costs roughly 10% to 14% of the price, and from 2021 to 2025, St. Louis County prices rose about 5% a year per square foot, so it took about two to three years of appreciation just to cover the round trip. How do physician mortgages work?

They let doctors buy with little or nothing down and no private mortgage insurance, often qualifying on a signed employment contract and treating student loans more favorably than a conventional loan. Is a St. Louis home a good investment for a physician?

Treat it as a place to live that can also build equity, not as your best investment. Holding it costs about 0.8% to 1.1% of the price a year in property taxes alone near the hospitals, plus insurance and repairs, and older houses need more. Should you buy or rent for the years you’ll be here?

Start with how long you’ll stay. Buying and later selling a St. Louis house costs roughly 10% to 14% of the price, so the house has to appreciate enough to cover that before buying beats renting on resale alone.

  • A fellowship or anything under about three years: renting usually wins. The round trip eats the equity.
  • Residency of four years or more: buying can work with a physician loan and a house that resells easily, but plan your exit before you buy.
  • An attending position you expect to keep: this is where buying usually makes sense.

How do physician mortgages change the math?

They change the cash you need, not the price of the house. Most programs share four features, but the details vary by lender:

  • Low or no down payment, often in tiers. One lender, for example, lists 0% down up to $1 million, 5% up to $1.5 million and 10% up to $2 million.
  • No private mortgage insurance, sometimes in exchange for a slightly higher rate than a conventional loan.
  • Qualifying on a signed employment contract that starts within roughly 60 to 90 days of closing.
  • More flexible student-loan treatment in the debt-to-income calculation, depending on deferment or an income-driven payment.

Low down payment doesn’t mean low cash: closing costs still run 2% to 3%, and a small down payment means a bigger loan and less equity if you sell early. Compare at least two lenders on the rate, the down-payment tier for your price and exactly how your student loans are counted. What St. Louis closing costs include →

The contract feature only helps employed doctors. If you own your practice, are buying into a partnership or work locums on a 1099, the lender reads your last two tax returns instead, and every write-off lowers the income it counts. How practice owners and partners qualify →

What does a St. Louis home cost to hold as an asset?

More than the mortgage. Near the hospitals, the median property tax ran about 0.8% to 1.1% of the sale price in 2026, from about $4,400 a year in Ballwin to $13,700 in Ladue. Add insurance, and budget for upkeep, because much of this housing is older: 66% of Kirkwood’s 2026 sales and 89% of Webster Groves’ were built before 1960.

Can the house become a rental later?

Sometimes, but run the rental math before you count on it. From 2023 to 2025 I sold 130 homes for an investor team, and whether a house works as a rental comes down to the same test every time: the rent, minus taxes, insurance, repairs, vacancy and management, measured against what you paid.

A house bought as a family home near the hospitals, at those prices and tax bills, has a high bar to clear. If keeping it as a rental is part of your plan, price that rent before you buy, not when you’re relocating. How investors price a house →

What should you ask your financial advisor before you buy?

Bring the house numbers to the person who sees the rest of your finances. Six questions to start with:

  1. How long do we realistically expect to stay, and what happens to this purchase if we leave early?
  2. How does a physician loan with little down compare with a larger down payment for our cash flow and investments?
  3. How do our student loans and repayment plan affect what we should borrow?
  4. What total monthly cost, including taxes, insurance and upkeep, fits our plan?
  5. How much cash should we keep in reserve after closing for repairs on an older house?
  6. If we might rent the house out later, how would that fit our taxes and overall plan?

This page covers the real estate side only. It isn’t investment, tax or legal advice; your advisor and lender should run your own numbers.

More Questions Physicians Ask

Residency, loans and contracts

Should I buy a house during residency?

Only if you’ll stay long enough. Buying and later selling costs roughly 10% to 14% of the price, so a residency of four years or more in a house that resells easily is where buying can work. For a shorter program, renting usually wins. A physician loan can qualify you on your contract with little down. Buy or rent by timeline ↑

Is a physician loan worth it?

It’s worth it when keeping cash invested or in reserve matters more than the slightly higher rate some lenders charge. Little or no down payment and no private mortgage insurance let you buy sooner, but a small down payment means a bigger loan and less equity if you sell in the first few years. What to compare between lenders ↑

What is the difference between a physician loan and a conventional loan?

A conventional loan with less than 20% down requires private mortgage insurance and counts student loans under standard rules. A physician loan usually skips mortgage insurance with 0% to 10% down, can qualify you on a signed employment contract and treats student loans more flexibly, sometimes at a slightly higher rate. Physician loan features ↑

Can I use a physician loan with an employment contract that hasn’t started yet?

Often, yes. Many physician loan programs qualify you on a signed contract for a job that starts within about 60 to 90 days of closing. Lenders verify the salary and start date, and a delayed start can affect the approval. How physician loans qualify you ↑
