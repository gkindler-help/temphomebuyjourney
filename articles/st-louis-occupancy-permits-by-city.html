Do You Need an Occupancy Inspection to Sell? In St. Louis, It Depends on the City

Last updated October 30, 2026

In St. Louis, the rules for selling a house change at the city limit. Unincorporated St. Louis County requires an occupancy permit when a new occupant moves in; Crestwood and Chesterfield don’t require one to sell an existing home; Maryland Heights inspects at every transfer. Here’s which cities require what, who orders it, and how to keep it from delaying your closing.

Occupancy Permits in St. Louis, Answered

Do I need an occupancy permit to sell a house in St. Louis County?

It depends on the city. Unincorporated St. Louis County, Sunset Hills, Green Park, Webster Groves and Maryland Heights require one when a house changes hands. Crestwood, Chesterfield and the City of Fenton don’t require one to sell an existing owner-occupied home. The city-by-city table ↓

Who orders the occupancy inspection, the buyer or the seller?

Usually the seller. In Sunset Hills the seller commonly applies before closing, and Lakeshire tells the seller to request the inspection before closing. The contract can assign it differently, so read yours. Who orders it ↓

How long does an occupancy inspection take?

Plan on weeks, not days. Maryland Heights can take a few weeks, and St. Louis REALTORS warned in 2024 that County inspection waits could reach four to six weeks. Order it as soon as you decide to sell. How to keep it off the critical path ↓

Does my mailing address tell me which occupancy rules apply?

No. A Fenton, Florissant or Webster Groves mailing address doesn’t mean the house is inside that city. Check the county’s map or property record for the actual municipality. The mailing-address trap ↓

Is the occupancy inspection the same as a home inspection?

No. The city checks its own list; your home inspector checks the house for you. On a 2025 Northwoods purchase, the city’s failure list and the buyer’s inspection report didn’t cover the same problems. Occupancy list vs. inspection report →

Who orders the occupancy inspection, the buyer or the seller?

The seller, in most cities. The permit is usually tied to the property changing hands, and the city wants the inspection done before the new occupant moves in. Sunset Hills sellers commonly apply before closing; Lakeshire tells the seller to request the inspection before closing; Marlborough requires written disclosure of its re-occupancy requirement before the sale.

Some towns allow a conditional occupancy that shifts the corrections to the buyer by agreement. If you’re buying, find out before you sign who pays for anything the city fails.

How long does an occupancy inspection take, and how do you keep it from delaying closing?

Order it the day you decide to sell. Inspection waits run in weeks: St. Louis REALTORS warned in 2024 that County waits could reach four to six weeks, and many County municipalities use County inspectors for their own inspections. Then any failed items have to be fixed and reinspected; in unincorporated St. Louis County, the owner has 90 days after the first inspection to make the repairs. Some approvals expire: a Sunset Hills application is good for up to 6 months, and Byrnes Mill’s and Rock Community Fire Protection District’s inspections are good for 6 months.

How it went on a real sale: Maryland Heights inspects at every transfer, and I had one done on both of my 2026 sales there. The city can be behind. If you’re selling in Maryland Heights, schedule the occupancy inspection as soon as you plan to sell. Maryland Heights rules →

How do you know which city’s rules apply to a house?

Look up the municipality, not the mailing address. Post office names cross city limits: a house with a Florissant address can sit in unincorporated St. Louis County, and “Fenton” can mean the City of Fenton, unincorporated St. Louis County or Jefferson County, each with different occupancy rules. The St. Louis County map and property record show which municipality a parcel is in.

How it went on a real sale: 12945 Covington Gardens Drive had a Florissant mailing address but sat in unincorporated St. Louis County, so the County’s occupancy rules applied, not Florissant’s. That sale → · The three Fentons →

What happens if the house fails the occupancy inspection?

The listed items have to be corrected and reinspected before the permit is issued, unless the city allows a conditional occupancy that moves the corrections to the buyer by agreement. Failed items become part of the sale: who fixes them, who pays and whether closing waits.

For a buyer, the city’s list is a floor, not a home inspection. Put it next to your own inspection report so you know which repairs the occupancy work covers and which need their own agreement. How that comparison went on a real purchase →

Which St. Louis County towns have sewer-lateral programs?

Several small South County towns run residential sewer-lateral programs, including Bella Villa, Grantwood Village, Marlborough, Shrewsbury and Wilbur Park. Programs and rules vary, so ask the town for the current details. South County small towns →
