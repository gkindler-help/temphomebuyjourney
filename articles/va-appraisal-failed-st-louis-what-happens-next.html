VA Appraisal Failed in St. Louis: What Happens Next (And Your 3 Options)

Last updated October 23, 2026

The VA appraisal came back with conditions, or with a value below your price. The loan can’t close as things stand. Here are your three options, what protects your earnest money, and what to do first.

VA Appraisal Problems Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first. These cover a VA appraisal that comes back with repair conditions or a value below the contract price.

What happens if a VA appraisal comes back with repair conditions?

The loan can’t close until they’re cleared. You have three paths: the seller makes the repairs, you pay for them and close, or you end the deal under your contract. Which one works depends on the cost, the seller and your contract deadlines.

Does the seller have to make VA appraisal repairs?

No. A seller can agree, refuse or negotiate. Small items like a handrail or a patch of peeling paint often get done. A $2,000 to $4,000 panel replacement or a roof usually turns into a negotiation over who pays.

Can I get my earnest money back if the VA appraisal fails?

If the problem is value, yes: the VA escape clause in the required VA rider lets you walk without losing your earnest money when the price is more than the VA’s appraised value. If the problem is repair conditions the seller won’t make, your protection comes from the St. Louis REALTORS contract’s financing terms and the VA rider, so read them with your agent and follow the deadlines exactly. Earnest money moves by mutual release, a form both sides sign.

What if the VA appraisal comes in below the price?

The VA escape clause covers you: if the price is more than the VA’s appraised value, you aren’t obligated to buy and you don’t lose your earnest money. You can also renegotiate the price or pay the difference in cash. A VA loan won’t finance the gap. The opposite surprise is easier to use. When a 2025 Cedar Hill VA appraisal came in $10,100 over the contract, we restructured the price and a seller credit, and the buyer brought $1,879.01 to closing. Can I pay for the VA repairs myself and close?

Yes, if you have the cash and the house still makes sense at that cost. Ask your lender how they want the work documented before you pay anyone. Don’t drain your emergency fund or borrow to cover appraisal repairs.

How fast do I need to decide?

Before your contract’s deadlines pass. Get contractor bids the day the appraisal comes back, send them to the seller and negotiate. If the seller won’t fix the issues and you can’t pay for them, act inside the deadline, not after it.

Your Three Options

Option 1: The seller makes the repairs

This is the cleanest outcome. The seller hires the work, the lender gets proof it’s done, and you close. It happens most when the seller wants to close and the repair is small. Get contractor bids right away and send them over: a seller who sees a $2,800 bid is more likely to agree than one looking at a vague repair condition.

A strong appraisal doesn’t take repairs off the table, either. On a Northwoods VA purchase the appraisal came in $15,100 over the price and still required a furnace reinspection, with the roof, sink, termites and a blocked sewer line on the buyer’s inspection besides. How those were negotiated after the appraisal →

If the seller agrees but needs more time, both of you have to sign an extension. Extensions carry risk: the work can run late, your rate lock can expire, and the seller can change their mind.

Option 2: You pay for the repairs and close

This makes sense when the repairs are small and you have the cash, or when the price leaves enough room that the repair still pencils out. It doesn’t make sense if it drains your emergency fund. A VA loan won’t add the repair cost to your loan.

Option 3: You end the deal

When the seller won’t fix the issues and you can’t pay for them, the deal ends and you start over. It feels like a loss, but the appraisal did its job: it kept you from buying a house with unresolved safety problems at a price that didn’t account for them. You won’t get back what you spent on the appraisal and the inspection.

How Your Earnest Money Is Protected and Released

In St. Louis we use the St. Louis REALTORS residential sale contract, and on a VA purchase the VA rider is required. It spells out your protections as the borrower, including the VA’s escape clause: if the price is more than the VA’s appraised value, you aren’t obligated to buy and you don’t lose your earnest money. Read the VA rider with your agent before you sign, so you know your deadlines and the steps for giving notice.

Earnest money moves by mutual release, a form both sides sign. If they don’t agree, Missouri law requires the broker holding it to turn the disputed funds over to the State Treasurer’s Unclaimed Property Division within 365 days of the projected closing date (RSMo 339.105.4). More VA Appraisal Problems Questions

What if the seller offers to split the repair cost?

That can work if you have the cash for your half. On a $4,000 repair split evenly, you need $2,000 at closing. If you have it and the house is worth it, it’s a reasonable compromise.

Can the lender increase my loan to cover the repairs?

Not on a standard VA loan. The repairs have to be completed, or the condition cleared, before closing.

What if the seller won’t release my earnest money?

Earnest money moves only by a release both sides sign. If the seller won’t sign, Missouri law sets how the broker holding it handles the disputed funds (RSMo 339.105.4). Following your contract’s deadlines and notice steps exactly is what keeps you on solid ground.
