When Is the Best Time to Buy, Sell or Move in St. Louis?

Last updated October 27, 2026 · George Kindler, Licensed Missouri Real Estate Agent

People ask me this every year, and the honest answer depends on which side of the deal you’re on. The good news is that St. Louis County runs on a calendar you can see coming.

I pulled five years of monthly county sales, 2021 through 2025, plus 2026 through September. The same pattern shows up every year: buyers crowd in during March and April, prices peak on June and July closings, and the market slows down from November through February.

Sellers want the crowd. Buyers who can wait want the quiet months. Movers are fighting the summer calendar.

Best Time to Buy, Sell or Move, Answered

What is the best time to sell a house in St. Louis?

List in March or April. Across 2023–2025, St. Louis County homes averaged 10.5 to 10.8 showings per listing in March and April, about twice December’s 5.1, and went under contract in a median 6 to 7 days from April through July. Showings peaked in March or April in all five years from 2021 to 2025. If you have to sell before you buy, I usually suggest listing at the end of winter, around February. Why spring favors sellers ↓

What is the best time to buy a house in St. Louis?

November through January, if you want the least competition. Showings per listing average 5.1 to 7.9 those months, roughly half the spring peak, and homes sit a median 14 to 22 days, about three times longer than in spring. The tradeoff is choice: December brings less than half of May’s new listings. How buyers use the slow months ↓

When are St. Louis home prices lowest?

In homes that close in January and February, which were mostly negotiated in November and December. Price per square foot ran about 9% below its yearly trend in January and about 9% above it in June, from 2021 to 2026. The median sale price swings more, about 35%, because bigger, pricier homes sell in summer. How much cheaper winter really is ↓

When do homes sell fastest in St. Louis?

April through July. The median home went under contract in 6 to 7 days those months across 2023–2025, compared with 19 to 22 days in December through February. See every month ↓

When do most people move in St. Louis?

More closings happen June through August than any other time of year. Those months held about 10% of each year’s closings apiece across 2023–2025, compared with 5.4% in January. A financed sale usually closes 30 to 45 days after the contract, so a summer move starts with a spring contract. Planning a move date ↓

Spring Is When Sellers Have the Most Buyers

March and April averaged 10.5 and 10.8 showings per listing across 2023–2025. December averaged 5.1. That’s twice as many buyers walking through each house in spring.

Homes listed into that crowd move fast. From April through July, the median home went under contract in 6 to 7 days. Contracts written in April and May close in June and July, which are the peak price months every year.

Here’s how I’d use that:

  • Be on the market by March or April if your timeline is flexible. That’s when the most buyers are looking at each listing.
  • If you have to sell before you buy, list around February. You catch the demand as it builds, without carrying two mortgages, and you can buy as spring inventory comes on.
  • Don’t waste the first weekend. In a busy spring market, buyers who don’t like your price simply buy the next house. What your first weekend is telling you →

Spring also brings the most competing listings. New listings climb from 6.2% of the year’s total in January to 10.2% in May. Your house is competing with more houses, so price and condition matter more, not less.

Fall and Winter Give Buyers Room to Negotiate

From November through January, showings per listing averaged 5.1 to 7.9, about half the spring peak. Homes sat a median 14 to 22 days, roughly three times as long as in spring. A house that has been on the market for three weeks in December is a normal winter listing, not a problem house.

The catch is selection. December brought 4.8% of the year’s new listings, less than half of May’s 10.2%. You’ll see fewer houses, so you need to know what you want before the right one shows up.

How I’d time a purchase depends on the buyer:

  • Stretching your budget in a competitive price range? Wait for fall, or look at listings that have sat.
  • A strong borrower in a price range with options? Fall or spring can both work.
  • Buying and selling at the same time? Buy in spring and sell in summer. Is now a good time to buy →

How Much Cheaper Is Winter, Really?

The median sale price makes winter look like a clearance sale: about $219,000 on January closings and $311,000 on June closings, averaged over 2023–2025. Most of that is not the same house getting cheaper. More of the bigger, pricier homes sell in summer, and smaller homes make up more of the winter sales.

Price per square foot takes out most of the size difference. I compared each month with that year’s overall price trend, so normal appreciation doesn’t make December look more expensive than January:

January and February closings ran about 9% below trend, and June closings about 9% above. That’s a real seasonal swing of roughly 20%, not the 35% the median price suggests. Even price per square foot still mixes in condition and location, and summer sales lean toward move-in-ready homes, so treat 20% as the upper end.

What this means for a buyer: a similar house negotiated in November or December and closed in January has typically sold for noticeably less per square foot than one negotiated in April and closed in June. Not 35% less, but enough to matter, and with less competition.

Summer Is Moving Season

June, July and August each held about 10% of the year’s closings across 2023–2025. January held 5.4%. More St. Louis moves happen in summer than in any other season, because spring is when the most contracts are written.

If you need a specific move date, work backward. A financed sale usually closes 30 to 45 days after the contract, so a mid-July move means a contract in late May or early June. If your date is flexible, January and February have the fewest closings of the year.

What These Numbers Can’t Tell You

These are countywide medians. Your price range and school district can run on a different clock:

  • Price range: in 2026, homes under $200,000 drew far less competition than homes from $250,000 up. Competition by price range →
  • School district: showings by month varied widely across the county’s 22 districts in 2026. Month by month for each district →
  • The house itself: a move-in-ready home in a popular area can draw multiple offers in January, and an overpriced one can sit in April.

Use the calendar to set expectations. Use the comparable sales and the first weekend to set the price.

More Questions About Timing a Move

Timing your sale

When are the most homes for sale in St. Louis?

New listings peak in May, about 10% of the year’s total, and bottom out in December at 4.8% (2023–2025 averages). More listings mean more choice for buyers, but they also mean more competition for sellers.

When should I list if I’m selling and buying at the same time?

If you need to sell first, I usually suggest listing at the end of winter, around February. If you’re buying first, buy in spring and sell in summer. Buying and selling at the same time →

Should I wait until spring to sell?

If your timeline allows it, spring brings more buyers and more room for mistakes. If you’re relocating, carrying two houses or need to move, a winter plan built around your timeline is the better answer. Selling in fall or winter →

Reading the numbers

Why does the median price drop so much in winter?

Partly because different homes sell. From 2021 to 2026 the median sale price swung about 35% between January and June closings, but price per square foot swung about 20%. Larger and more expensive homes make up more of the summer sales, which pulls the summer median up.

Does the closing month tell you when the deal was made?

No. A financed sale usually closes 30 to 45 days after the contract, so June closings were mostly negotiated in April and May, and January closings in November and December. Plan around the contract month, not the closing month.

Will this pattern hold next year?

The timing has held every year since at least 2021: showings peaked in March or April, prices peaked in June or July, and the longest market times came in December or January. Rates and inventory move the overall level from year to year, so read it as a pattern, not a forecast.

Is spring or fall better for buyers?

Fall and winter bring less competition; spring and summer bring more homes and more competition. Which fits you depends on your budget and price range. Is now a good time to buy →
