How Much House Can You Afford on $150K in St. Louis at 7%?

Last updated October 2, 2026

On a $150,000 salary, a comfortable St. Louis budget is about $435K, with lender approval up to about $568K. That covers most of West County — Webster Groves, Eureka and Fenton fall below your comfortable number, Ballwin and Kirkwood line up with it, and Chesterfield East is within reach. At this income the decisions are about down payment, what you're selling, and resisting the pull of the maximum.

The Math: $150K Salary → $435K–$568K Home

Lenders start from your gross monthly income — $12,500 on $150,000 — and apply two limits: what your housing payment can be, and what all of your debts together can be. The low end of the range keeps your full payment at 28% of gross income. The high end is what most lenders would approve at a 43% total debt-to-income ratio, assuming about $800 a month in other debts (car, student loans, credit cards).

$150K: Down Payment Strategy Matters More Than Approval

Approval is rarely the hurdle at $150K — the question is how much to put down. This page assumes 10% down on a conventional loan. Here's how the choice changes a $500K purchase:

  • 5% down: about $40,000 to close, with higher PMI.
  • 10% down: about $65,000 to close, with lower PMI.
  • 20% down: about $115,000 to close, with no PMI at all.

If you're selling a home, timing matters. Buying first may require a bridge loan or carrying two payments; selling first may mean a short-term rental. Many buyers at this level make offers contingent on their sale, which is weaker in a 5-day market — talk through the sequence before you start touring.

Example: Relocating to St. Louis on $150K

Illustrative example, not a client story. A household moving from a higher-cost city earns $150,000, has $800 a month in car payments, and has $140,000 in savings.

  • Comfortable target: about $435K. Many relocating buyers are surprised how far that goes here.
  • Lender's likely maximum: about $568K.
  • Cash to close at $500K with 20% down: about $115,000. That fits their savings but leaves only about $25,000 in reserve, so 10% down ($65,000) may be the smarter choice.
  • Payment at $500K with 10% down: about $4,027 a month.

Mistakes at $150K

Buying the maximum because "it's cheap compared with where we came from." The payment still has to fit your St. Louis budget.

Choosing a ZIP code for its schools without checking the address. ZIP codes span multiple districts here. Verify every address.

Ignoring reassessment. St. Louis County reassesses property in odd-numbered years, so plan for your tax bill to change.

Making a sale-contingent offer in a 5-day market without a plan to make it competitive.

$150K Affordability Questions

How much house can I afford on $150K in St. Louis?

About $435K comfortably and up to about $568K with typical debts, using a conventional loan with 10% down at the rate assumed on this page.

Which West County areas fit a $150K salary?

Based on recent median sale prices, Webster Groves, Eureka, Ballwin and Kirkwood fall at or below the comfortable budget, and Chesterfield East is within the lender's range.

Should I put 20% down on $150K?

Only if you'll still have three to six months of reserves. 10% down with PMI is often the better balance.

How These Numbers Are Calculated

  • Interest rate: 7% on a 30-year fixed loan, in line with the market as of October 2, 2026. Your rate depends on credit, loan type and the market on the day you lock.
  • Property tax: 1.12% of the price for the budget range and payment table (common in St. Louis County); the area table shows each ZIP’s rate.
  • Insurance: about 1% of the home’s price per year. Missouri premiums vary widely — get quotes early.
  • Mortgage insurance: FHA 0.55% per year plus 1.75% upfront (financed); conventional PMI estimated at 0.60% with 5% down and 0.40% with 10% down.
  • Limits: comfortable = total housing payment at 28% of gross income; lender maximum = 43% total debt-to-income, minus the other debts stated.
  • Closing costs: estimated at 3% of the price.
  • Area data: median sale price, days on market and first-weekend sale rate from 7,006 residential sales, 2025–2026, MARIS MLS.
