Can You Buy a House With a Federal Pacific Panel Using a VA Loan?

Last updated October 23, 2026

Yes, you can buy a house with a Federal Pacific panel using a VA loan, but plan on the panel being replaced. The real questions are who pays for it, about $2,000 to $4,000 in 2026, and whether it happens before closing.

Can you buy a house with a Federal Pacific panel using a VA loan?

Yes, but plan on replacing the panel. The VA requires an electrical system that’s safe, an appraiser who notes a Federal Pacific panel can condition it, and many insurers won’t write a new policy on one. The useful question is who pays for the replacement and whether it happens before closing.

How do I identify a Federal Pacific panel?

Open the panel door. Look for Federal Pacific or FPE branding on the door or label, “Stab-Lok” printed on the breakers, and breakers with orange or blue tips. The panel is usually in the basement, garage or utility room, often near where the electrical service enters the house.

How much does it cost to replace a Federal Pacific panel in St. Louis?

About $2,000 to $4,000 in 2026, more if the service needs to be upgraded at the same time. Get an electrician’s estimate before you write the offer so you know the number you’re negotiating over.

Will insurance cover a home with a Federal Pacific panel?

Many insurers won’t write a new policy on one. Get a homeowners insurance quote on the house during your inspection period, before that deadline passes, so a coverage problem doesn’t surface at closing.

Which St. Louis homes are most likely to have one?

Homes built from about 1950 to 1980, the years these panels were installed. In 2026, 54.5% of St. Louis County sales were built from 1950 to 1979, and the share ran from 3.4% in the Clayton School District to 96.5% in Riverview Gardens. What should I put in an offer on a house with a Federal Pacific panel?

A requirement that the seller replace the panel through a licensed electrician before the appraisal, with the electrician’s estimate attached so the seller knows exactly what they’re agreeing to. If the seller won’t, decide whether you’ll pay for it yourself or move on.

How to Spot One at a Showing

  • Federal Pacific or FPE branding on the panel door or inside label
  • “Stab-Lok” printed on the breakers
  • Breakers with orange or blue tips

If you can’t find the panel, ask the listing agent where it is before the showing. If you’re not comfortable opening the door, ask your agent to. Touring a house with one? Flag the panel in the walk-through tool →

What Happens If the Appraisal Flags It

The appraisal will condition the panel, and the loan can’t close until the condition is cleared. Ask your lender what proof of completion they need. From there you have three options: the seller replaces it, you pay for it and close, or you end the deal under your contract. Most sellers won’t pay for a surprise $2,000 to $4,000 repair, which is why finding the panel before you write matters. Your three options when a VA appraisal fails →

Writing the Offer

If you want the house, write the replacement into the offer: the seller replaces the panel through a licensed electrician before the appraisal, and the electrician’s estimate is attached. A seller whose house has sat for weeks is more likely to agree. Get your insurance quote during the inspection period, so you know before that deadline whether the panel affects your coverage.

If the seller says the panel was already replaced, ask for the electrician’s paperwork and check permit records with the city or county.

More Federal Pacific Panels and VA Loans Questions

Why are Federal Pacific panels a problem?

Their Stab-Lok breakers can fail to trip under overload, which creates a fire risk. That’s why inspectors call them out, appraisers can condition them and many insurers won’t write a new policy on a house that has one.

What about Zinsco panels?

Treat them the same way. My repair cost guide lists Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels together as needing replacement, at about $2,000 to $4,000 in 2026.
