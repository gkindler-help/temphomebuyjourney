We Bought Their Next House Before Selling the First. Here’s How We Managed Both Closings.

Last updated October 28, 2026

How do you buy your next house before you sell the one you’re in? In June 2025, my brother-in-law wanted 5941 Creek Side Place in Cedar Hill while he still owned 5154 Autumn Ridge Drive in Imperial. We bought first, with no home-sale contingency, and ran two contracts, two sets of inspections and two closings as one plan. Step into my seat, make the calls, then read how each one played out.

How do you buy your next house before selling the one you have?

You make the purchase stand on its own, then sell into it. Here the buyers’ VA financing didn’t depend on selling Autumn Ridge, so the Creek Side offer didn’t need a home-sale contingency. We got the purchase under contract first, listed Autumn Ridge five days later, and set the two closings six days apart.

The rest of this page walks through each side, and where the two plans had to meet.

Why offer without a home-sale contingency?

Because the buyers didn’t need one, and it made the offer stronger. They were preapproved up to $350,000 with VA financing, so we offered full price, $309,900, on June 7, ten days after Creek Side listed. The sellers accepted on June 8, with $2,000 earnest money and a July 10 closing.

A home-sale contingency tells a seller your purchase depends on another sale. Without one, this was a plain VA offer at full price. What a home-sale contingency costs you →

When do you list the house you’re leaving?

Once the purchase is under contract. Autumn Ridge went up as a coming-soon listing on June 12 and went active at $275,000 on June 13. On June 14 we had a signed contract at $290,000, with $5,000 in seller-paid closing costs and a July 16 closing.

What happens when both houses have inspections at once?

You negotiate two sets of findings in the same week, on opposite sides of the table. At Creek Side, as the buyer, the inspection found elevated radon among other items, and we negotiated a $1,000 radon-related concession; mitigation was arranged after closing.

At Autumn Ridge, as the seller, the buyers’ inspection found two actively leaking cracks in the front foundation wall. We lined up a waterproofing company, the seller chose to pay for the work at closing, and the seller credit went to $8,500 total. Both inspection periods were resolved by June 25.

What do you do when the VA appraisal comes in high?

Treat it as room to restructure, not free money. On June 30 the VA appraisal came in at $320,000 with no required repairs, $10,100 above the contract price. Raising the price also means financing more principal, so the question was how to use that room.

The first proposal was $317,000 with a $7,100 seller credit. The final amendment on July 9 set the price at $315,000 with a $5,650 seller credit, $5,000 below the appraisal. After the lender rebalanced the figures, the buyers’ cash to close came to $1,879.01. I stayed on the lender and the title company until the closing numbers matched the amendment.

Did they have to carry two mortgages?

Only for six days, and without a bridge loan or a HELOC. Creek Side closed and funded on July 10; Autumn Ridge closed on July 16 and paid off the old mortgage. The purchase didn’t wait on money from the sale: the buyers brought $1,879.01 to closing.

Setting the purchase closing first, with the sale closing a week behind it, meant they moved once and never had to find a place to live in between. Bridge loans, HELOCs and two payments →

What almost delayed the sale closing?

Title paperwork, not the buyers. Two items were still open in the last week. The HOA trustee letter needed a $125 payment, made July 10. And title needed a formal payoff statement for the old mortgage; the regular monthly statement wasn’t enough to calculate it.

The payoff statement arrived July 15. Title also had the waterproofing invoice and lien waiver. We did the final walk-through that evening, and Autumn Ridge closed and funded July 16.

What made buying first work?

Financing that didn’t depend on the sale, and treating two closings as one plan. Each side had its own problems: the appraisal and radon at Creek Side, the foundation cracks, HOA letter and payoff at Autumn Ridge. Each one needed its own follow-up, on a timeline that had to land in the right order.

What should buyers who also have a house to sell take from this?

Get the purchase financed on its own, list once it’s under contract, and run both closings on one calendar.

What has to be true to skip a home-sale contingency?

Your loan has to approve you for the new house without the old one selling. Here VA financing and a preapproval up to $350,000 made that possible, so the offer went in at full price with no contingency.

How far apart should the two closings be?

Close enough that you aren’t carrying both for long, with the purchase first so you have somewhere to go. Here it was six days: July 10 for the purchase, July 16 for the sale.

Is a high VA appraisal free equity?

No. It gives you room to negotiate the price and seller credit, but a higher price is more principal to finance. Here we settled at $315,000 with a $5,650 credit, $5,000 under the appraisal.

What paperwork can hold up the sale closing?

The mortgage payoff statement and any HOA letter. Title needs a formal payoff, not your monthly statement, so order it early.

Questions This Sale Helps Answer

Can you buy with a VA loan before selling your current home?

In this sale, yes. The buyers were preapproved up to $350,000 while they still owned Autumn Ridge, offered with no home-sale contingency, and closed six days before the sale.

What does title need to pay off the seller’s mortgage?

A formal payoff statement from the lender. On Autumn Ridge the regular mortgage statement wasn’t enough, and the payoff statement arrived the day before closing.

Do you need a bridge loan to buy before you sell?

Not always. These buyers bought first without a bridge loan or HELOC; their cash to close on the purchase was $1,879.01.
