Clayton vs Ladue Under $1 Million: How Much of the Market Could You Actually Shop?

Last updated October 7, 2026

George Kindler· Licensed Missouri Realtor· October 2026· For Buyers

A buyer tells me they want to stay under $1 million and they're considering Clayton or Ladue.

At first, that sounds like two versions of roughly the same search.

The 2026 sales data says otherwise.

In both school districts, $1 million is a substantial housing budget. But relative to what actually sold, that budget put buyers in different positions.

In Clayton, under $1 million was a relatively thin lower portion of the market. In Ladue, buyers had more opportunities below that ceiling—but $1 million still wasn't buying access to most of the market.

That's the distinction that matters if you're actually trying to find a house rather than comparing two median prices on a screen.

Clayton: $1 Million Was Below the Center of the Market

In my St. Louis County school-district analysis, I found 29 QA-clean single-family Clayton sales from January 1 through October 5, 2026.

The median sale price was approximately $1.25 million.

That immediately puts a $1 million ceiling into perspective.

You're not starting your search around the middle of the Clayton market. You're searching below it.

And the lower you move the budget, the more pronounced that becomes. In this dataset, none of Clayton's qualifying single-family sales closed at $500,000 or less.

That doesn't mean a home can never sell in the Clayton School District below $500,000. It means there wasn't one among the QA-clean single-family sales included in this particular 2026 study period.

The same caution applies at $1 million.

There were opportunities below that number, but the monthly data shows how thin that search could become.

Looking specifically at homes with 2+ bedrooms and an original list price of $999,999 or less, Clayton frequently produced only zero or one eventual closed-sale opportunity in a month.

That's a very different problem from simply asking whether your budget is high enough.

Your budget might work.

The question becomes how long you are willing to wait for something that fits it.

Ladue: More Room Below $1 Million, But Don't Mistake That for the Whole Market

Ladue looked different.

The full 2026 dataset contained 82 QA-clean single-family sales, with a median sale price of approximately $1,028,500.

That's much closer to the $1 million line than Clayton's $1.25 million median.

There was also measurable activity much farther down the price range. Approximately 12.2% of the Ladue sales in the dataset closed at $500,000 or less.

But there's an important second half to that story:

Under $1 million still wasn't most of the Ladue market.

So I wouldn't describe Ladue as an “under-$1-million market.” The data doesn't support that.

What the data does show is that a buyer with that ceiling generally had more room to operate below $1 million in Ladue than in Clayton.

The monthly Buyer Opportunity analysis reinforces that difference.

Using the same filter for both districts—2+ bedrooms with an original list price of $999,999 or less—Ladue produced multiple qualifying opportunities in more months.

Clayton's qualifying cohort was considerably thinner and frequently came down to zero or one opportunity in a month.

That matters when you're planning the search.

Same $1 Million Ceiling. Different Number of Chances.

This is where median price alone can hide something important.

A Buyer Opportunity in the monthly analysis means a property with 2+ bedrooms and an original list price of $999,999 or less that entered the market during that month and later closed within the study cohort. It is not a live inventory count, and it isn't every listing that came to market.

What it helps us see is the frequency with which a buyer fitting that search would have encountered eventual successful transactions.

And that's where Clayton and Ladue start to separate.

A $1 million buyer might be financially capable of buying in either district.

But that doesn't mean the market is going to give that buyer the same number of chances.

What Does That Mean for the Actual Home Search?

If I were starting with “Clayton or Ladue, under $1 million,” I wouldn't immediately ask which district is better.

I'd ask better housing questions.

How specific are you about the house?

If you need a particular number of bedrooms, lot size, condition or architectural style, every additional requirement can make an already-thin pool smaller.

How long are you willing to wait?

A budget that occasionally works is different from a budget that regularly produces choices.

What happens if nothing fits this month?

Can you wait?

Expand the search?

Adjust the house?

Or is the district itself the priority?

And finally:

Are you comparing what your budget can technically buy—or how frequently something you would actually want becomes available?

Those aren't the same thing.

That's especially important in Clayton.

A buyer shouldn't look at a $1 million budget and assume they're shopping the center of that market simply because $1 million is a large number.

The 2026 closed sales put that budget below Clayton's median.

Ladue gave the same buyer more room, but even there, $1 million didn't encompass most of the market.

Use the Data to Set Expectations, Not Pick a School District

This analysis isn't a ranking of Clayton and Ladue schools.

It's housing data.

My broader St. Louis County school district housing guide looks at 2026 closed sales across 22 districts and shows how much of each district's market different budgets could reach.

You can also use the West County guide to put higher-priced West County searches into a broader housing context.

And there's one more St. Louis County wrinkle worth repeating:

The city or mailing address does not necessarily tell you the school district.

If the district matters to your decision, verify the school assignment for the exact property address rather than relying on the municipality, ZIP code or listing headline.

If you're trying to work through a specific budget and housing combination, you can ask me.

The useful question usually isn't:

“Which district should I choose?”

It's:

“What would my actual search look like in each one?”

About the Data

This analysis uses QA-clean single-family closed sales from January 1 through October 5, 2026, using the same methodology as the STL Home Journey county school-district analysis.

The monthly comparison uses a narrower filter:

  • 2+ bedrooms
  • Original list price ≤ $999,999

“Buyer Opportunities” are qualifying properties listed during a given month that later closed within the study cohort.

They should not be interpreted as:

  • All listings
  • Current inventory
  • Live availability
  • Proof that no qualifying property existed during a blank month

Clayton's sample is particularly small, so individual months should be treated as evidence of the overall pattern rather than precise predictions of what a future month will produce.

Past closed sales also do not tell us what is available for sale today.
