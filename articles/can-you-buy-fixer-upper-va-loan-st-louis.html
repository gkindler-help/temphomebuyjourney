Can You Buy a Fixer-Upper with a VA Loan in St. Louis? The Honest Answer

Last updated October 23, 2026

Yes, if the work is cosmetic. No, if the house needs safety, structural or system repairs before you can live in it. Here’s where the line sits, what that means for as-is listings, and where VA buyers find real upside in St. Louis.

Fixer-Uppers and VA Loans Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first. “Fixer-upper” means different things to different buyers, and with a VA loan the line is cosmetic work versus repairs the VA requires before closing.

Can you buy a fixer-upper with a VA loan in St. Louis?

If the work is cosmetic, yes. If the house needs safety, structural or system repairs, those have to be done before closing, because a VA appraisal conditions anything that affects safety, soundness or sanitation. Dated kitchens, old carpet and worn floors are fine. A leaking roof, a Federal Pacific panel or a failing foundation isn’t, until it’s fixed.

What passes a VA appraisal and what doesn’t?

Cosmetic wear passes: dated finishes, old flooring, worn carpet, outdated fixtures, original cabinets and older appliances. What gets conditioned is anything that affects safety, soundness or sanitation, such as peeling paint on a pre-1978 house, missing handrails, roof leaks, a heating system that doesn’t work, plumbing leaks, electrical hazards and serious foundation or water problems.

Can I buy an as-is house with a VA loan?

Only if it already meets the VA’s property requirements, or the seller will make the repairs the appraisal requires. An as-is listing usually signals the seller won’t fix anything, so most aren’t a good fit for a VA buyer.

What is a VA renovation loan?

A VA loan that finances the purchase and the repairs together, based on the house’s value after the work. Few lenders offer it, and each sets its own rules on contractors, budget and timeline. Talk to a VA lender before you count on one.

More on renovation loans →

Can I do the repairs myself after closing?

The cosmetic ones, yes. Repairs the VA requires have to be done before closing, not after.

Can I offer less on a cosmetic fixer-upper?

Yes, if the comparable sales support it and you can show what the updates will cost. Make sure the house will pass the appraisal first: a seller won’t take a lower offer that also carries appraisal risk.

Where the Line Sits

Move-in ready doesn’t mean perfect. It means the house is safe, sound and sanitary on the day you close.

Passes: dated finishes, old flooring, worn carpet, outdated fixtures, faded paint on a newer house, popcorn ceilings, original kitchen cabinets, laminate countertops, older appliances, paneled walls and cosmetic bathroom tile.

Gets conditioned: peeling paint on a house built before 1978, missing or loose handrails, broken windows, roof leaks, heating that doesn’t work, plumbing leaks, electrical hazards such as a Federal Pacific panel, and serious foundation or water problems. The issues I see most →

Why As-Is Listings Rarely Work

A seller who lists as-is is telling you they won’t make repairs. That can be an estate, a bank or an owner who simply won’t. If the appraisal conditions repairs, the seller says no and the deal stalls. I’ve seen VA buyers write on as-is houses expecting to negotiate after the appraisal, and lose weeks.

VA Renovation Loans

A VA renovation loan finances the purchase and the repairs together, with the appraisal based on the value after the work. Lenders hold the repair money and release it as the work is completed and checked. Few lenders offer it, and each sets its own rules on contractors, budget and timeline, so get those rules in writing from a VA lender before you write an offer that depends on one.

For a true fixer-upper, conventional financing or cash usually gives you more room. Fixer-upper vs. move-in ready in St. Louis →

Where VA Buyers Find Upside

Look for houses that are sound but dated: the dated kitchen, old carpet and tired bathrooms that make other buyers move on. More Fixer-Uppers and VA Loans Questions

Can I use a VA loan to buy a foreclosure in St. Louis?

Only if it passes the VA appraisal or the bank will make the repairs the appraisal requires. Most bank-owned houses are sold as-is, and most banks won’t make repairs.

Are VA renovation loans worth it?

For most VA buyers in St. Louis, they’re hard to find and slow to close. If a house needs significant work, conventional financing or cash is usually the better fit.
