When a Deal Dies, the Earnest Money Isn’t Automatically Anyone’s

Seven signed releases, $5,725 to sellers, and one buyer who got it all back

Last updated October 29, 2026

Usually not. Most buyers who back out do it under a contingency, and their earnest money goes back to them. When a buyer doesn’t perform on the contract, the seller can keep it, but only through a release both sides sign. Here’s how that worked on real St. Louis deals.

Earnest Money in St. Louis, Answered

Does the seller keep the earnest money if the buyer backs out?

Typically, no. Most buyers who back out do it during their contingencies, and the money goes back to them. The seller keeps it when the buyer fails to meet their contingencies or doesn’t perform on the contract. Over 13 years, my sellers have kept tens of thousands of dollars that way. When does the seller keep the earnest money?

When the buyer doesn’t hold up their end. That usually means one of three things: they miss a contingency deadline, they back out for a reason the contract doesn’t give them, or they simply can’t close on time.

Even then, it isn’t automatic. The contract gives the seller the right to ask for it, and both sides have the right to ask the other to perform. The money moves when both sides sign a release. In every case below, that signed release is what put the money in the seller’s hands.

How much did my sellers actually keep in 2024 and 2025?

$5,725 across seven signed releases, from $500 to $1,500 each. These are the ones I’ve documented from the last two years; over 13 years, my sellers have kept tens of thousands of dollars.

When does the buyer get all of it back?

When they leave under a contingency on time, or when the problem is on the seller’s side. In March 2024, 6643 Chamberlain Avenue, listed around $165,000, went under contract with $1,500 earnest money. The title search turned up a mechanic’s lien of more than $40,000 and an open loan.

Before the buyer spent money on inspections, I asked the title company’s underwriter whether the lien would stop the sale. I didn’t want that buyer paying for an inspection on a house they couldn’t buy. A week later the contract ended in a mutual release, and all $1,500 went back to the buyer. The seller kept nothing.

Who decides where the earnest money goes?

The buyer and the seller, together. The money is held by the title company or the broker named in the contract, and it moves on a mutual release: one form that ends the contract and says who gets the deposit. Both sides sign it.

That’s why the release is the negotiation. On Blackforest, the buyer agreed to surrender the $725 and signed. On Chamberlain, the release sent it all back to the buyer. A request for the money isn’t a recovery; a signed release is.

What happens if the buyer and seller don’t agree?

The money stays put while they argue. If neither side signs a release, Missouri law requires the broker holding the funds to turn them over to the State Treasurer’s Unclaimed Property Division within 365 days of the initial projected closing date (RSMo 339.105.4).

So a fight over a 1% deposit can tie it up for a year, and it can tie up the house too. On a Maplewood sale, we were heading into earnest money dispute territory, and the seller chose to close instead. When it gets that far, talk to a real estate attorney, not just your agent.

How much earnest money protects a seller?

More than most sellers ask for. About 1% is typical for a financed buyer in St. Louis, and none of the deposits in the table above was more than $1,500. On cash deals I’ve always recommended 10%, because a cash buyer has no loan contingency and the deposit is most of the seller’s protection.

More Questions About Earnest Money

Situations

Can the seller keep the earnest money if the appraisal comes in low?

Not if the buyer has an appraisal contingency and uses it on time. Without one, a buyer who can’t close because of the appraisal can lose the deposit. On Hamlet Court, the buyer couldn’t close after the appraisal didn’t meet the lender’s criteria, and the signed release gave the seller $1,000. What a low appraisal does →

Does the buyer lose the earnest money if their loan falls through?

Not while the loan contingency is still open. After it ends, a buyer who can’t close can lose it. In November 2025 a buyer’s lender wasn’t ready by closing, the sale was cancelled, and the signed release gave the seller $500. All seven releases ↑

Can the seller keep the earnest money and sell to someone else?

Yes. The release that ends the first contract also settles the deposit, and the seller is free to close with a new buyer. On Hodiamont Avenue, the first buyer’s release gave the seller $500, and a replacement buyer closed and funded on December 19, 2024. The 2024–2025 releases ↑
