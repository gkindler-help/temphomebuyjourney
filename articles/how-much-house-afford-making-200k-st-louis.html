How Much House Can You Afford on $200K in St. Louis at 7%?

Last updated October 2, 2026

On a $200,000 salary in St. Louis, a comfortable budget is about $658K, and lenders would approve up to about $870K with typical debts. That's more than the median sale price in all but two ZIP codes in our data — Ladue and Chesterfield (63005). You're shopping the top of most markets, so the questions are about loan structure, carrying costs and how much house you actually want to own.

The Math: $200K Salary → $658K–$870K Home

Lenders start from your gross monthly income — $16,667 on $200,000 — and apply two limits: what your housing payment can be, and what all of your debts together can be. The low end of the range keeps your full payment at 28% of gross income. The high end is what most lenders would approve at a 43% total debt-to-income ratio, assuming about $1,000 a month in other debts (car, student loans, credit cards).

$200K: Jumbo Limits, Reserves and Carrying Costs

This page assumes 20% down on a conventional loan, which removes PMI. Two loan details matter at your price range:

  • Conforming limit: loans above the 2026 conforming limit of $832,750 are jumbo loans, with stricter credit, reserve and documentation requirements. With 20% down, you'd stay conforming up to a purchase price of about $1.04 million.
  • Reserves: lenders often require several months of payments in savings after closing at this level — and for a jumbo loan, often more.

Carrying costs also grow faster than the price. At $765K, property tax alone is about $714 a month, and insurance about $638 — plus maintenance on larger, older homes, which often runs 1–2% of the home's value each year.

Mistakes at $200K

Assuming the top of the market resells like the middle. Upper-end homes can take longer to sell and have fewer comparable sales — think about your exit, not just the purchase.

Borrowing just over the conforming limit. A slightly larger down payment that keeps you under $832,750 can avoid jumbo requirements entirely.

Underbudgeting for carrying costs. Taxes, insurance and maintenance on a larger home can add $1,500+ a month on top of principal and interest.

Skipping specialized inspections. Older Ladue and Kirkwood homes often need sewer scopes and structural or roof specialists beyond a general inspection.

$200K Affordability Questions

How much house can I afford on $200K in St. Louis?

About $658K comfortably and up to about $870K with typical debts, using a conventional loan with 20% down at the rate assumed on this page.

Will I need a jumbo loan on a $200K salary?

Only if your loan exceeds the 2026 conforming limit of $832,750. With 20% down, that's a purchase above about $1.04 million.

What areas fit a $200K salary in St. Louis?

Most of West County. Ladue's median matches the comfortable budget, Chesterfield (63005) reaches toward the stretch budget, and areas like Kirkwood, Des Peres and Ballwin fall below it.

How These Numbers Are Calculated

  • Interest rate: 7% on a 30-year fixed loan, in line with the market as of October 2, 2026. Your rate depends on credit, loan type and the market on the day you lock.
  • Property tax: 1.12% of the price for the budget range and payment table (common in St. Louis County); the area table shows each ZIP’s rate.
  • Insurance: about 1% of the home’s price per year. Missouri premiums vary widely — get quotes early.
  • Mortgage insurance: FHA 0.55% per year plus 1.75% upfront (financed); conventional PMI estimated at 0.60% with 5% down and 0.40% with 10% down.
  • Limits: comfortable = total housing payment at 28% of gross income; lender maximum = 43% total debt-to-income, minus the other debts stated.
  • Closing costs: estimated at 3% of the price.
  • Area data: median sale price, days on market and first-weekend sale rate from 7,006 residential sales, 2025–2026, MARIS MLS.
