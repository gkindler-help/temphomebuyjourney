What South County Actually Is

South County is the informal name for the southern portion of St. Louis County. It is not a municipality, a school district, or a zip code. It is a geographic and cultural shorthand that buyers, agents, and longtime residents use to describe a cluster of communities south of I-44 and the River Des Peres that share a broadly suburban character and similar daily-life infrastructure.

The nine communities in this guide — Affton, Concord, Crestwood, Green Park, Lemay, Mehlville, Oakville, Sappington, and Sunset Hills — are what most buyers mean when they say "South County." They span a median home value range from $152,000 to $523,000. They are served by three school districts. And they sit in different positions on the competitiveness spectrum — some of the hottest markets in the entire state of Missouri, and some of the most patient.

South County vs. South City South City refers to the southern portions of the City of St. Louis — a separate political entity with its own tax structure, school system, and housing stock. South County is South St. Louis County. They share a border but are different markets. If you are searching both, understand you are searching two distinct markets.

School Districts: The Decision That Changes Everything

Before you decide on a neighborhood, decide whether school district matters to you — and if it does, understand that district assignment in South County is an address-level question, not a neighborhood-level question.

The three districts serving South County buyers are Lindbergh Schools (93.5% APR score), Mehlville School District (91.7% APR score), and Affton School District. Both Lindbergh and Mehlville are top performers. The difference shows up in the price premium: Lindbergh-assigned communities command a meaningful premium over comparable Mehlville-assigned homes.

Which Buyer Profile Fits Which Community

The most common mistake South County buyers make is starting with a community name instead of starting with their actual situation. Here is a cleaner way to think about it:

  • First-time buyer, budget under $200K: Lemay and Affton are the realistic options. Both reward buyers who inspect carefully and buy for condition over brand name.
  • First-time or move-up buyer, $200K–$280K: Mehlville is the primary market. High competition — you need pre-approval and a willingness to move quickly.
  • Move-up buyer, $280K–$330K, Lindbergh priority: Sappington is the underrated option. Less competition than Crestwood, same district.
  • Move-up buyer, $280K–$330K, flexible on district: Concord and Oakville both sit in this range. Concord in the Lindbergh footprint commands a premium; Oakville gives you more inventory in the Mehlville district.
  • Move-up buyer, Lindbergh and ready to compete: Crestwood is the target — but understand it is the hottest market in Missouri. You will compete.
  • Premium buyer, $450K+: Sunset Hills is the South County option. Larger lots, lower density, Lindbergh Schools, and a price point that filters the buyer pool significantly.

Occupancy Permits in South County: Why the Address Matters

“South County” is a regional label, not one permitting jurisdiction. Occupancy rules are set by each incorporated city or village, and by St. Louis County for unincorporated areas. Two homes a few blocks apart can fall under different rules. A community name or mailing address doesn’t by itself tell you which jurisdiction’s rules apply; the exact address does.

Do I need an occupancy permit when I buy a home in South County?

It depends on which jurisdiction covers the address. Some South County cities and unincorporated St. Louis County require an occupancy permit or inspection when a home sells or a new occupant moves in. Crestwood does not require one for an owner-occupied resale, and Fenton has no general resale occupancy permit for existing homes. Use the table below for the jurisdiction that actually covers the property.

Do occupancy rules differ between South County municipalities?

Yes. The trigger differs: some jurisdictions tie the permit to any change of occupancy, some to a change of ownership, some to rentals only, and Fenton to new dwellings. Who applies and when also differs. In Sunset Hills the seller commonly applies before closing, and in Lakeshire the seller is instructed to request the inspection before closing.

How do I find out which occupancy rules apply to a specific address?

Before closing or moving in, confirm the rule for the specific property:

  1. Look up the exact address in the St. Louis County Geospatial Data Center to see whether it is inside a city or village or in unincorporated St. Louis County.
  2. Open the official occupancy source for that jurisdiction (linked in the table) and note what triggers the requirement: a sale, a change of occupant or a rental.
  3. Ask who applies and when. In some cities the seller typically applies before closing; in others the new occupant obtains the permit.
  4. Build the inspection and permit into the closing or move-in date. In unincorporated St. Louis County, the home can’t be occupied until the permit is issued.
  5. If the home will be rented, confirm the rental process separately. Crestwood, for example, asks for the rental application at least 30 days before move-in.

Before You Start Searching

Three things matter more than picking a neighborhood name:

  1. Know your actual budget — not what you're approved for, but what the monthly payment looks like at current rates including property taxes, insurance, and PMI if applicable. South County property tax rates vary significantly by zip code and municipality.
  2. Get pre-approved before you tour. In Crestwood, Mehlville, and Affton especially, the window between seeing a home and needing to write an offer can be 24 to 48 hours. Buyers who tour without pre-approval lose deals.
  3. Verify school district by address before you fall in love with a home. In Concord especially, the district can shift block to block. The listing description is not a reliable source.

South County FAQ

What are the best neighborhoods in South County St. Louis?

It depends entirely on your budget and priorities. Crestwood and Sunset Hills are the premium Lindbergh options. Oakville and Concord offer strong value in the $300K–$320K range. Mehlville and Sappington are the mid-range. Affton is the most practical entry point. Lemay offers the most accessible pricing. There is no single best neighborhood — there is the right one for your specific situation.

How much do homes cost in South County St. Louis?

South County home prices run from approximately $152,000 median in Lemay to $523,000 in Sunset Hills. The middle of the market — Oakville, Concord, Crestwood — runs in the $300K–$320K range. These are census median values; actual current sale prices vary.

Is South County St. Louis a competitive real estate market?

Yes. Crestwood ranks #1 most competitive in all of Missouri. Mehlville ranks #5 with 68.6% of homes selling above list price. Affton is #7 and Oakville #11. Pre-approval and a strategy are required — not optional — before touring in South County.

What is the difference between South County and South City?

South City is the southern portion of the City of St. Louis — a separate political and tax jurisdiction. South County is South St. Louis County, which includes the communities in this guide. They share a border but are distinct markets with different tax structures, school districts, and housing stock profiles.
