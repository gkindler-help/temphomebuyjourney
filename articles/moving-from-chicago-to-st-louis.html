Moving From Chicago to St. Louis: Find the Area With the House You’re Leaving

Last updated October 30, 2026

Most guides to moving from Chicago to St. Louis compare the cost of living. This one compares houses. Find the kind of house you’re leaving, then see which St. Louis areas have the most of that same era and style, with the 2026 sales behind it.

Moving From Chicago, Answered

Which part of St. Louis is most like the Chicago suburbs?

It depends on the house you’re leaving. 1960s–70s ranches like Arlington Heights and Palatine match Mehlville, Concord and Oakville. Larger two-stories like Naperville match Chesterfield and Wildwood. Brick bungalows match Maplewood and South City, and North Shore homes match Ladue and Clayton. The match table ↓

Where are ranch houses in St. Louis like the northwest suburbs?

In South County. In 2026 sales, 60.1% of Mehlville High-area homes and about half in Concord and the Oakville High area listed a ranch style, with medians built from 1954 to 1979. Arlington Heights, Palatine and Hoffman Estates run from 1974 to 1976. South County ranch areas ↓

Where should I look in St. Louis if I’m leaving a Naperville two-story?

Chesterfield and Wildwood in West County. Their 2026 sales had median sizes of 3,326 and 3,342 sq ft. Chesterfield’s median house was built in 1982 and Wildwood’s in 1992, close to Naperville’s 1989. Larger two-stories ↓

Does St. Louis have brick bungalows like Chicago?

Yes. Chicago’s bungalows were built mostly from 1910 to 1940, and St. Louis built brick in the same years. In 2026 sales, the median house was built in 1929 in Maplewood and 1935 in University City, and South City is full of 1910s–40s brick bungalows and flats. Brick bungalows ↓

What is the St. Louis version of the North Shore?

Ladue and Clayton at the top, with 2026 medians of $1,430,000 and $1,240,000, then Kirkwood at $566,750 and Webster Groves at $444,500. Like Winnetka, Glencoe and Highland Park, they aren’t one price band. Established suburbs ↓

Leaving a 1960s–70s ranch or split-level?

Look at South County first. The northwest suburbs are one of Chicagoland’s densest belts of postwar ranches and split-levels: the median house was built in 1974 in Arlington Heights, 1976 in Palatine and Hoffman Estates, and 1979 in Schaumburg. In St. Louis, the same era and style is concentrated in Mehlville, Concord, Oakville and Affton.

Leaving a larger 1980s–2000s two-story?

Look at West County, and decide whether you need newer or just larger. Naperville’s median house was built in 1989, with 52.1% built from 1980 to 1999, according to CMAP’s housing profile; Orland Park (1989) and Tinley Park (1987) are similar, and Plainfield is newer, at 2003.

Leaving a Chicago brick bungalow?

Look at South City and the older inner suburbs. Chicago’s bungalow belt holds more than 80,000 bungalows built mostly from 1910 to 1940, by the Historic Chicago Bungalow Association’s count, nearly a third of the city’s single-family houses. St. Louis built brick in the same years.

In 2026 sales, Maplewood’s median house was built in 1929, at 1,344 sq ft and a $295,000 median price; University City’s was built in 1935, at 1,815 sq ft and $439,200. In the City, South City neighborhoods such as Lindenwood Park, Bevo and Tower Grove South are built largely of 1910s–40s brick bungalows and flats. If you’re leaving a Chicago two-flat, the St. Louis version is the South City brick flat. South City neighborhoods →

Leaving an established, higher-priced suburb?

Look at Ladue, Clayton, Kirkwood and Webster Groves, and expect tiers, not one price band. Chicago Magazine’s 2025 rankings put the median single-family sale at $1.98 million in Winnetka, $1.70 million in Glencoe, $1.38 million in Lake Forest and $855,600 in Highland Park. St. Louis has a similar spread.

What changes, even when the house looks the same?

The rules around the house. Four things catch Chicago buyers off guard:

  • The city may not be the city. St. Louis City and St. Louis County are separate, the County has 88 municipalities, and much of South County is unincorporated. City vs. County, explained →
  • The school district may not match the town. District lines don’t follow city or ZIP lines here, so look up the district for the exact address. School district map →
  • Some cities inspect before a sale. Many St. Louis County cities require an occupancy inspection when a house sells, and each sets its own rules. Occupancy permits by city →
  • The tax math changes. Compare the tax bill on the exact listing, not the list price alone, and know that working in St. Louis City adds a 1% earnings tax. The earnings tax →

Whatever the age of the house, get a sewer scope. I recommend one on every St. Louis home; it runs $150 to $300. What’s different about St. Louis houses →
