Moving From Nashville to St. Louis: Find the Area With the House You’re Leaving

Last updated October 30, 2026

Nashville’s suburbs are newer than most of St. Louis, and one of them shares a name with a very different St. Louis suburb. Here’s how to translate: pick the Nashville-area town you’re leaving, and see which St. Louis areas have houses of the same age, size and price, from 2026 sales.

Moving From Nashville, Answered

Which St. Louis suburbs are most like Nashville’s?

Go by town. Brentwood, Tennessee points to Wildwood and Town and Country, Franklin to Wildwood, Murfreesboro to Jefferson County’s newest towns, Hendersonville to Oakville and Arnold, and East Nashville to the South City bungalow streets. Town-by-town matches ↓

Is Brentwood, Missouri like Brentwood, Tennessee?

No. Brentwood, Tennessee is a large-lot suburb with a 1996 median house. Brentwood, Missouri is a small, older inner suburb: in 2026 sales in its school district, the median house was built in 1940, at 1,204 sq ft and $401,000. The St. Louis match for Brentwood, Tennessee is Wildwood or Town and Country. The two Brentwoods ↓

Where should I look in St. Louis if I’m leaving Franklin?

Wildwood. About three-quarters of Franklin’s housing went up from 1990 on, and Wildwood’s 2026 sales had a 1992 median at 3,342 sq ft. For something newer, the Jefferson County side of Eureka had a 2021 median. Franklin’s matches ↓

What does a Murfreesboro budget buy in St. Louis?

Newer houses in Jefferson County at similar prices. In 2026 resales, Pevely’s median was $272,000 for a 2001 house, Herculaneum’s $319,900 for a 2009 house and Imperial’s $354,500 for a 1999 house. Murfreesboro’s matches ↓

Will I pay state income tax in St. Louis?

Yes. Tennessee doesn’t tax wages; Missouri does, and a job inside St. Louis City adds a 1% earnings tax wherever you live. Taxes and other changes ↓

Leaving Brentwood, Tennessee?

Skip Brentwood, Missouri; it isn’t the same kind of place. Brentwood, Tennessee is 87.8% detached houses with a 1996 median, according to Census data, grown on large lots since the 1970s. Brentwood, Missouri is a small inner suburb of older houses: its school district’s 2026 sales had a 1940 median at 1,204 sq ft and $401,000.

Leaving a Franklin subdivision?

Wildwood is where to start. Franklin built 23.1% of its housing in the 1990s, 21.3% in the 2000s and 28.1% in the 2010s, with a 2002 median, according to Census data, around a small historic downtown. Wildwood’s houses date mostly from the same 1990s build-out, with a 1992 median.

For a house built in the last decade, the Jefferson County side of Eureka had a 2021 median in 2026 resales, at $469,900 for about 2,100 sq ft across 23 sales. Ask about the HOA either way; many St. Louis subdivisions have one, but the amenities rarely match a Franklin master plan. Eureka’s Jefferson County side →

Leaving Murfreesboro?

Look at Jefferson County’s newer towns, which compete on price. Murfreesboro has the Nashville metro’s newest big-city housing: 55.6% built since 2000 and a 2002 median, according to Census data. Its St. Louis counterparts are towns that grew in the same years at similar prices.

Leaving a Hendersonville house?

Oakville, Arnold and High Ridge line up best. Hendersonville’s core went up from the 1960s through the 1980s, 47.1% of its housing, with a 1989 median and a newer 2010s layer, according to Census data. Oakville’s 2026 median was 1979, Arnold’s 1987 and High Ridge’s 1988.

Leaving East Nashville or a downtown condo?

For bungalows, look at South City; for a condo, the Central West End. East Nashville’s East End is a pre-war core, 23.8% built before 1940, with a 2010s infill wave of 26.0%, according to Census data. St. Louis’s South City neighborhoods kept more of their originals: Tower Grove South, Lindenwood Park and Shaw are lined with 1910s–40s brick bungalows and flats.

If you’re leaving a Gulch or downtown tower, the Central West End is the walkable St. Louis neighborhood with the most condo buildings, between Forest Park and the medical campus. Every City address pays the 1% earnings tax and is in the St. Louis Public Schools district. South City neighborhoods →

Coming from a crawlspace: what does a St. Louis basement add?

A floor you probably didn’t have. Middle Tennessee’s shallow limestone makes digging basements costly, so most Nashville-area houses sit on a crawlspace or slab. A lot of the St. Louis ranches and two-stories in the tables above have a full basement, and the square footage on the listing counts only the floors above it.

That’s storage, a workshop, a family room once it’s finished, and a place to wait out a tornado warning. Comparing square footage and checking a basement →

What changes when you move from Nashville to St. Louis?

Mostly the tax mix. Four things to plan for:

  • A state income tax. Tennessee doesn’t tax wages; Missouri does, and working in St. Louis City adds a 1% earnings tax. How the earnings tax works →
  • A different property tax formula. Tennessee assesses houses at 25% of appraised value; Missouri assesses residential property at 19%, then applies local rates. Compare the actual bill on the listing, not the rate. St. Louis County tax bills by district →
  • More governments per mile. St. Louis County has 88 municipalities plus unincorporated areas, and school district lines don’t follow them. City vs. County, explained →
  • An inspection before a sale. Many County cities require an occupancy inspection when a house sells. Occupancy permits by city →

And scope the sewer line: I recommend it on every St. Louis house, at $150 to $300.
