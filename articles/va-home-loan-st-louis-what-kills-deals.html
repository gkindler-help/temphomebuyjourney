VA Home Loan in St. Louis: What Kills Deals (And How I Screen Properties)

Last updated October 23, 2026

Most VA deals that fall apart in St. Louis don’t fail on the buyer. They fail on the house. A VA appraisal checks condition as well as value, and older St. Louis homes carry the issues it catches. Here’s what kills deals, what protects your earnest money, and how I screen a house before we write the offer.

What kills VA deals in St. Louis?

Property condition, mostly. A VA appraisal checks the house against the VA’s Minimum Property Requirements as well as its value, and it conditions anything that affects safety, soundness or sanitation. In St. Louis the usual problems are older electrical panels such as Federal Pacific, peeling paint in homes built before 1978, missing or loose handrails, worn-out roofs, and foundation or water problems. When the seller won’t fix a condition, the deal stalls.

Is a VA appraisal stricter than a conventional appraisal?

On condition, yes. Like FHA, the VA holds the house to a higher safety standard than a conventional loan, and the two sets of guidelines are essentially the same. A conventional buyer can often take a worn roof as-is and negotiate a credit. On a VA loan, required repairs have to be cleared before the loan can close.

What protects my earnest money on a VA purchase in St. Louis?

The contract and the VA rider. In St. Louis we use the St. Louis REALTORS residential sale contract, and on a VA purchase the VA rider is required. It spells out your protections as the borrower, including the VA’s escape clause: if the price is more than the VA’s appraised value, you aren’t obligated to buy and you don’t lose your earnest money.

Does a VA loan make my offer weaker in St. Louis?

Not on a house in good condition. VA buyers usually have more room to get a loan commitment than FHA buyers. The risk is the property: a house with deferred maintenance or a seller who won’t address appraisal conditions turns the VA loan into a problem. That’s why I screen the house before we write.

Can I buy a fixer-upper with a VA loan?

If the work is cosmetic, yes. If the house needs safety, structural or system repairs before you can live in it, those have to be done before closing.

How do I avoid a VA deal that won’t close?

Screen the house at the showing, before you write. Open the electrical panel, walk the outside for peeling paint, check the stairs and handrails, look at the roof from the ground and go to the basement. If you find a problem, write the repair into the offer or move on.

The Five Issues I See Most

These are the conditions that come up most on VA appraisals of St. Louis homes. Costs are 2026 expected costs from my St. Louis repair cost guide.

1. Federal Pacific and other older electrical panels

Federal Pacific Stab-Lok panels went into many homes from the 1950s to the 1980s, and their breakers can fail to trip under overload. The VA requires an electrical system that’s safe, an appraiser who notes one can condition it, and many insurers won’t write a new policy on one. Replacement runs about $2,000 to $4,000. Federal Pacific panels and VA loans →

2. Peeling paint in homes built before 1978

Homes built before 1978 are presumed to have lead-based paint, and on those homes the VA requires peeling, chipping or flaking paint to be corrected. Check window sills, door frames, porch railings, exterior trim and basement stairwells.

3. Missing or loose handrails

A missing or loose handrail on porch, deck or basement stairs is a safety item an appraiser can condition. It’s a small fix, but I’ve seen one delay a closing when the seller wouldn’t do it or couldn’t get it done in time.

4. Roofs near the end of their life

The VA requires a roof that keeps moisture out and has reasonable life left. Missing shingles, sagging or active leaks get conditioned. A conventional buyer might take the roof as-is with a credit; a VA buyer can’t close until it’s resolved. Replacement runs about $8,000 to $20,000.

5. Foundation and water problems

Hairline cracks are common in St. Louis basements. Bowing walls, large cracks or active water can lead to a structural engineer’s evaluation before the loan moves forward. Minor foundation repairs run about $3,000 to $8,000; major ones $15,000 to $40,000 or more.

How I Screen a House Before We Write

I don’t wait for the appraisal to find problems. On every VA showing I check the electrical panel, the exterior paint, the stairs and handrails, the roof from the ground and the basement walls and floor. If I see an issue, I tell my buyer before we write. We either write the repair into the offer or move on to the next house. If the Appraisal Comes Back With Conditions

The loan can’t close until the conditions are cleared. The seller can make the repairs, you can pay for them and close, or you can end the deal under your contract. More VA Home Loans in St. Louis Questions

How common are Federal Pacific panels in St. Louis?

They show up most in homes built from about 1950 to 1980, and 54.5% of 2026 St. Louis County sales were built from 1950 to 1979. See the share by school district →

Can I ask the seller to fix issues before the appraisal?

Yes. If you see a problem at the showing, write the repair into your offer and attach a contractor’s estimate so the seller knows what they’re agreeing to. Sellers usually agree to minor items, or to bigger ones when they’re motivated to sell.

Do I still need a home inspection with a VA loan?

Yes. The VA appraisal isn’t a home inspection; it looks for property-requirement problems, not everything a house might need. I recommend a full inspection, a radon test and a sewer scope on every St. Louis home, whatever its age.
