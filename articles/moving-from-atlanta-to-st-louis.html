Moving to St. Louis

Moving From Atlanta to St. Louis: Find the Area With the House You’re Leaving

Last updated October 30, 2026

Atlanta and St. Louis both have mature trees, hills and suburbs that grew in waves, so the houses can feel familiar. What differs is where each era ended up. Match the decade and style of your Atlanta house to the St. Louis areas built in the same years, using 2026 sales here and Census figures there.

Moving From Atlanta, Answered

What part of St. Louis feels like the Atlanta suburbs?

The answer changes by decade. Alpharetta’s 1990s two-stories line up with Wildwood, Marietta’s 1980s houses with Arnold and High Ridge, Woodstock’s 2000s subdivisions with Hillsboro and Fenton, and Decatur’s bungalows with Kirkwood, Webster Groves and Maplewood. Decade by decade ↓

Where is the St. Louis version of Alpharetta?

Wildwood, in West County. Alpharetta’s median house dates from 1996, and more than half its housing went up in the 1980s and 1990s; Wildwood’s 2026 sales had a 1992 median and 3,342 sq ft above ground. Alpharetta’s match ↓

Can I find a 2000s-built house like Woodstock’s in St. Louis?

Yes, mostly in Jefferson County. Woodstock’s median house dates from 2005; in 2026 resales, Pevely’s median was 2001, Hillsboro’s 2002, Fenton’s Jefferson County side 2003 and Herculaneum’s 2009. Woodstock’s matches ↓

Is there a St. Louis suburb like Decatur?

Kirkwood and Webster Groves come closest: walkable downtowns, older houses and a school district that shares the city’s name, the way Decatur runs its own system apart from DeKalb County. Maplewood is the smaller, lower-priced version. Decatur’s matches ↓

What’s the St. Louis equivalent of Buckhead?

It depends which Buckhead. For a detached house on a big lot, Ladue and Clayton, with 2026 medians of $1,430,000 and $1,240,000. For a high-rise condo, the Central West End and Clayton. Buckhead’s matches ↓

Leaving a 1990s Alpharetta two-story?

Tour Wildwood first. Alpharetta isn’t new construction: its median house dates from 1996, 56.6% of its housing went up in the 1980s and 1990s, and only 2.6% since 2020, according to Census data. Wildwood was built in the same years.

Leaving a 1980s Marietta house?

Look across the Meramec, in Jefferson County. Marietta’s median house dates from 1987, and the 1980s are its biggest single decade, a quarter of its housing, according to Census data. Arnold and Festus have the same 1987 median, at prices around $300,000.

Leaving a 2000s–2010s Woodstock home?

Jefferson County again, a little farther south. Woodstock is Atlanta’s newer-exurb story: 42.6% of its housing went up in the 2000s and another 25.6% in the 2010s, for a 2005 median, according to Census data. St. Louis County has little of that age, so the closest matches are these Jefferson County towns.

Leaving Decatur or an intown bungalow street?

Start with Kirkwood and Webster Groves. Decatur mixes a pre-war core, nearly a third of its housing built before 1950, with a wave of 2010s infill, according to Census data, and Virginia-Highland’s bungalows went up from 1909 to the 1930s, per the Atlanta History Center. St. Louis’s walkable older suburbs are mostly the original houses.

Leaving Buckhead or Midtown?

Decide which Buckhead you’re leaving. Only 26.5% of Buckhead’s homes are detached houses, and 47.0% are in buildings of 50 or more units, according to Census data, so the estate reputation describes a minority of its homes.

For a detached house on a large lot, look at Ladue, with a 2026 median of $1,430,000 for about 4,400 sq ft, and Clayton, at $1,240,000 for about 3,600 sq ft. For a high-rise or condo, the Central West End puts Forest Park and the hospitals within walking distance, and Clayton has condo towers in its office district. Clayton vs. Ladue under $1 million →

Will a St. Louis house have a basement like mine?

Quite possibly. In Atlanta, it depends on the lot: daylight basements on sloped north-side lots, crawlspaces under many older intown houses, slabs on flat subdivision lots. In St. Louis, a lot of the ranches and two-stories in the tables above sit on a full basement whether the lot slopes or not.

If you’re leaving a crawlspace or slab house, that basement is extra room the listed square footage doesn’t count. Comparing square footage and checking a basement →

What changes when you move from Atlanta to St. Louis?

Less than you’d expect about the houses, and more about the map. Four things to know:

  • Many more school districts. Metro Atlanta’s big districts follow county lines, Decatur aside. St. Louis County is split among many districts whose lines don’t follow city or ZIP lines, so check the district for the exact address. School district map →
  • Many more cities. St. Louis County alone has 88 municipalities and large unincorporated areas, each with its own rules. City vs. County, explained →
  • An inspection before a sale. Many County cities require an occupancy inspection when a house changes hands. Occupancy permits by city →
  • An earnings tax in the City. Georgia and Missouri both tax income, but a job inside St. Louis City adds 1% more. The earnings tax →

Older houses here mean older sewer lines. I recommend a sewer scope on every St. Louis home; it runs $150 to $300.

More Questions From Atlanta Movers

Before you move

Can I buy a St. Louis house without leaving Atlanta first?

Yes. Atlanta is a long drive or a short flight away, so you can tour on live video, rely on a full inspection report, and close remotely through the title company. Buying from out of state →

Do St. Louis subdivisions have HOAs like Atlanta’s?

Some do, especially newer ones, but many older St. Louis neighborhoods have no HOA at all. Ask for the HOA documents, dues and what they cover before you compare monthly costs. Where to live in St. Louis →
