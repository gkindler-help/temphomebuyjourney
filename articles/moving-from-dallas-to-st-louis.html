Moving to St. Louis

Moving From Dallas to St. Louis: Find the Area With the House You’re Leaving

Last updated October 30, 2026

Most guides to moving from Dallas to St. Louis compare the cost of living. This one compares houses. Find the kind of house you’re leaving, then see where St. Louis has the most of that same era and size, with the 2026 sales behind it. Treat each match as a place to start, not an exact twin, and count the basement: many St. Louis houses have a whole level under them that a Dallas slab house doesn’t.

Moving From Dallas, Answered

Which part of St. Louis is most like the Dallas suburbs?

It depends on the house you’re leaving. Plano two-stories point to Wildwood and Chesterfield, new Frisco and McKinney homes to Jefferson County’s newer communities, Richardson and Garland ranches to Oakville and Mehlville, and the Park Cities to Ladue and Clayton. The match table ↓

Where should I look in St. Louis if I’m leaving a Plano house?

Wildwood and Chesterfield. Plano’s median house was built in 1993, and Wildwood’s 2026 sales had a 1992 median at 3,342 sq ft. Chesterfield’s houses are about as large, 3,326 sq ft, but a decade older, with a 1982 median. Plano-era two-stories ↓

Does St. Louis have new construction like Frisco and McKinney?

Less than Dallas, and most of it is outside St. Louis County. In 2026 resales, the median house was built in 2021 on the Jefferson County side of Eureka, 2009 in Herculaneum and 2002 in Hillsboro. In St. Louis County, 17.5% of Oakville’s sales were built in 2000 or later. Newer communities ↓

Where are ranch houses in St. Louis like Richardson and Garland?

In South County and parts of West County. Garland’s median house was built in 1978 and the Richardson Heights area’s in 1973; Oakville’s 2026 sales had a 1979 median, Manchester’s 1973 and Mehlville’s 1966. Ranch areas ↓

What is the St. Louis version of the Park Cities?

Ladue and Clayton at the top, with 2026 medians of $1,430,000 and $1,240,000, then Kirkwood at $566,750 and Webster Groves at $444,500. Like Highland Park, they are older, established and mostly built out. Established neighborhoods ↓

Do St. Louis houses have basements?

Many do, and that’s space a Dallas house doesn’t have. Most Dallas-area houses sit on a concrete slab, so everything is above ground. A St. Louis ranch or two-story often has a full level underneath for storage, a rec room, a workshop or a place to shelter in a storm. What a basement adds ↓

Leaving a newer Frisco or McKinney home?

Look south, at Jefferson County. Frisco and McKinney are mostly new: Frisco’s median house was built in 2009 and McKinney’s in 2007, with about four in five of Frisco’s homes built since 2000, according to Census data. Most of St. Louis County was built decades earlier, so the region’s newest resale stock is in Jefferson County.

Leaving a 1960s–70s Richardson or Garland ranch?

Look at South County and the older parts of West County. Garland’s median house was built in 1978, and the Richardson Heights area’s in 1973; Richardson as a whole runs later, at 1986, because of newer infill. In St. Louis, Oakville is the closest match on age, and Manchester matches Richardson Heights.

Leaving Highland Park, University Park or Preston Hollow?

Look at Ladue, Clayton, Kirkwood and Webster Groves, and expect tiers, not one price band. Highland Park is a pre-war core with a steady wave of rebuilds: 27.5% of its housing was built before 1940, according to Census data. St. Louis’s established suburbs are older still on the whole, and mostly built out.

Leaving an Uptown condo or townhome?

Look at the Central West End and Clayton. The Central West End is the walkable St. Louis neighborhood most like Uptown: restaurants, Forest Park and the medical campus within a few blocks, with condos, townhouses and some houses. Clayton is the business district, with condo buildings close to its offices. Older brick neighborhoods such as Lafayette Square and Soulard offer townhouse-style living near downtown.

If you’re buying a condo, read the HOA documents before you compare monthly costs. Where to live in St. Louis →

What does a basement add if you’re used to a slab?

A whole extra level. Most Dallas-area houses are built on a concrete slab because of North Texas clay soil, so a 2,000 sq ft Dallas house is 2,000 sq ft of everything you get. Many St. Louis houses, including a lot of the ranches and two-stories above, sit on a basement: a full level underneath the main floors.

That space is a bonus a Dallas buyer isn’t used to counting. Unfinished, it’s storage, laundry, a workshop and the place to go when the tornado sirens sound. Finished, it can be a family room, guest room, office or gym. A St. Louis ranch with a basement can hold as much as a Dallas two-story, with the bedrooms all on one floor.

Two things to check when you compare. First, ask whether the listing’s square footage includes the finished basement or only the floors above it, so you compare like with like. Second, look at the basement closely: water stains, foundation-wall cracks and the sewer line all show up there, which is why I recommend a sewer scope on every St. Louis home. What changes when you move from Dallas to St. Louis?

The ground, the taxes and the map. Five things catch Dallas buyers off guard:

  • A basement to inspect. The extra level comes with its own checks: water, foundation walls and the sewer line. What a basement adds →
  • State income tax. Texas has none; Missouri does, and working in St. Louis City adds a 1% earnings tax. The earnings tax →
  • Property tax bills. Compare the tax bill on the exact listing, not the list price alone. St. Louis County tax bills by district →
  • Cities, not ISDs. You’re used to school districts that don’t follow city lines; St. Louis is the same, plus the County has 88 municipalities and large unincorporated areas, each with its own rules. City vs. County, explained →
  • Inspections at a sale. Many St. Louis County cities require an occupancy inspection when a house sells. Occupancy permits by city →

Whatever the age of the house, get a sewer scope. I recommend one on every St. Louis home; it runs $150 to $300.
