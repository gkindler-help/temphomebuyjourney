Check the price

Will it appraise?

“I love it. But is it actually worth that?”

Asking price and value are not the same thing. Before you decide what to offer, look at what comparable homes, the condition of the house and the local market say about the price. If an appraisal comes in below the contract price, the gap has to be resolved before closing.

Check the house

Will it pass inspection?

“The pictures look great. What am I not seeing?”

A home inspection isn’t technically pass or fail. What matters is what it uncovers, what those findings could cost, and whether they change what you’re willing to pay for the house. Listing photos show finishes. They don’t show the electrical panel, the major systems, the sewer line or septic system, or how a lender’s appraiser will view any of them. That doesn’t mean the house has a problem. It means these are worth checking before you’re under contract, while you still have options.

Check the address

What comes with the address?

“It’s a great house. What don’t I know about where it sits?”

Buying a house also means buying into everything attached to its address. Some of it changes across surprisingly short distances: two houses on the same block can be in different school districts, and a mailing-address city isn’t always the municipality that sets the rules.

  • A school boundary
  • A municipality, or unincorporated St. Louis County
  • Local occupancy and inspection requirements
  • Utilities: public sewer or septic
  • A neighborhood

Local rules and requirements

Occupancy, municipal inspection and sewer rules depend on the exact address, not the mailing city. Before you write an offer, confirm whether the property sits in a municipality or in unincorporated St. Louis County, because that decides whose rules apply.

Check the payment

What will it really cost?

“I can afford the listing price. Can I afford owning this particular house?”

The purchase price tells you what it costs to buy the house. It doesn’t tell you what it costs to own it. The real number includes the monthly payment, closing costs at the table, repairs the inspection turns up, and ongoing ownership costs like taxes, insurance and maintenance. Assistance programs can cover part of the upfront cost, if you qualify and the paperwork lines up.

Check the financing

Will the loan go through?

“I’m pre-approved. Isn’t that the hard part?”

Pre-approval is about you. Before closing, the lender also has to accept the house. The appraisal has to support the price, and FHA and VA loans carry property requirements that can flag issues a conventional loan might not. If you’re using MHDC assistance, its income limits and deadlines sit on top of the loan.

The other question

Shouldn’t your agent help you catch this?

Yes. Finding the listing isn’t the hard part anymore. Buyers can find houses from their phones. An agent’s value shows up after you find one you like:

  • Asking better questions about the house and the address
  • Noticing things that deserve a closer look
  • Weighing the asking price against appraisal risk
  • Understanding financing and property requirements
  • Verifying local details like school boundaries and occupancy rules
  • Structuring the offer around what you already know
  • Explaining what inspection findings mean for the deal

None of that replaces a licensed home inspector, an appraiser, your lender, an attorney, a contractor or an engineer. An agent’s job is to help you recognize the questions, keep the process coordinated, understand what findings mean for the transaction, and know when you need one of those specialists.

Aren’t all agents basically the same?

Don’t judge an agent by the sales pitch. Ask what they actually do after you find a house you like:

  • What do you look for when we walk through a house?
  • How do you help me decide whether the asking price makes sense?
  • How do you handle appraisal risk?
  • How do you verify school boundaries and local requirements?
  • What property issues could affect my financing?
  • What happens if inspection changes how I feel about the house?

The answers tell you more than a sales pitch will.
