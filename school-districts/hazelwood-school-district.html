Hazelwood School District: What Home Buyers Need to Know

Hazelwood School District is a big district, and that matters when you're looking at homes in North St. Louis County. It serves about 16,356 students across roughly 32 schools, with boundaries covering portions of Florissant, Hazelwood, Spanish Lake and surrounding unincorporated areas.

From a home-buying standpoint, the first thing I'd understand is that "Hazelwood School District" doesn't tell you the whole school story. Unlike a district such as Pattonville, which has one comprehensive high school, Hazelwood has three: Hazelwood Central, Hazelwood East and Hazelwood West.

So if you're looking at homes in Hazelwood School District, there are really two questions to answer. Is the property actually inside the district, and which Hazelwood high school does that particular address feed into?

That's important because municipal boundaries, mailing addresses and school district boundaries don't line up perfectly in St. Louis County. A Florissant address, for example, doesn't automatically tell you the school district or high school assignment. Verify the exact property rather than making the decision from the city name on the listing.

High schoolsThe three Hazelwood high schools

Hazelwood School District has three comprehensive high schools: Hazelwood Central, Hazelwood East and Hazelwood West. In a district this large, knowing which one serves a particular property is an important part of researching the address.

  • Hazelwood Central High School is located on New Halls Ferry Road in the Florissant area. It traces its history to 1954, when it opened as the original Hazelwood High School, and serves roughly 1,780 students.
  • Hazelwood East High School is located on Dunn Road in the Spanish Lake area. It was established in 1974 and serves roughly 1,310 students.
  • Hazelwood West High School is located on Wildcat Lane in Hazelwood and serves roughly 2,040 students.

The district offers Advanced Placement coursework across its high schools, but I would research the individual school if a specific AP course, academic program or extracurricular opportunity matters to your family. District-wide availability doesn't necessarily mean every program is identical at every high school.

The bigger real estate issue is the attendance boundary. I wouldn't try to determine whether a house feeds Hazelwood Central, East or West based only on whether the listing says Florissant, Hazelwood or another community. School attendance boundaries can cross municipal lines, and boundaries can change.

If attending a particular Hazelwood high school matters to your purchase, use Hazelwood School District's official boundary or school locator for the exact property address. If the assignment is important enough that it affects whether you'd buy the house, I'd verify it directly with the district before making that decision. I also wouldn't rely exclusively on the school information automatically populated on Zillow, Realtor.com, the MLS or another real estate website. Those are useful starting points. The district itself is the better source for confirming the current assignment.

PerformanceHow does Hazelwood School District perform?

Hazelwood School District is fully accredited by the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Missouri DESE).

That's an important fact because buyers sometimes encounter older school information online and assume it reflects the district's current standing. When accreditation or state performance matters to your decision, use current information from Missouri DESE and the district rather than relying on an older article or discussion. Hazelwood's current 2024-25 DESE Annual Performance Report score: [VERIFY].

I wouldn't substitute an older APR number here. Missouri's accountability system has changed over time, and older scores aren't necessarily comparable with results under the current system.

GreatSchools can be another useful research source, particularly when you're trying to organize information about the individual schools associated with an address. I just wouldn't turn one GreatSchools number into the entire decision. Look at the information behind the rating. Compare it with Missouri DESE's official reporting. If a particular program matters, check with the school or district directly.

That's especially important in a district with roughly 16,000 students and around 32 schools. A Hazelwood School District rating is a district-level data point. It doesn't tell you everything about Hazelwood Central, East or West, and it definitely doesn't tell you everything about a specific elementary or middle school. My preference is to start broad and then get specific: district accreditation, district performance, individual school information and finally the programs or services that actually matter to your family.

CommunitiesWhat communities are in Hazelwood School District?

Hazelwood covers a large footprint in North St. Louis County. Its boundaries include portions of Florissant, Hazelwood, Spanish Lake and surrounding unincorporated St. Louis County.

That creates one of the most common problems buyers run into when searching North County school districts: the city name on a listing doesn't necessarily identify the school district. Florissant is a good example. Seeing "Florissant, MO" in the address doesn't automatically mean Hazelwood School District. Different school district boundaries exist throughout this part of St. Louis County, so you need to check the specific property.

The same principle applies around the edges of Hazelwood's attendance area. School boundaries don't have to follow municipal boundaries, ZIP codes or the labels buyers use when searching for homes online. If you're specifically searching for homes in Hazelwood School District, I'd use the district as a search criterion but still verify each serious property independently. My basic process is simple:

  • Confirm the property is actually inside Hazelwood School District.
  • Use Hazelwood's official locator to verify the schools assigned to the exact address.
  • Research the assigned elementary, middle and high school individually.
  • Review current Missouri DESE information rather than relying on old district statistics.
  • Verify any specific academic program directly with the district or school if it's important to your decision.

That process takes a little more time, but it's better than finding a house you love and discovering later that you made an incorrect assumption about the school assignment.
