Who it's forBuyer fit for Mehlville School District

Mehlville is for buyers who want South County space, value, and flexibility without paying the full premium for Lindbergh or Webster. It fits first-time buyers, VA buyers, families upsizing from smaller homes, and anyone who wants a functional neighborhood more than a status ZIP code.

What you're paying forValue breakdown

You are paying for a large South County district with a wide range of homes, neighborhoods, and price points. The value is that buyers can find ranches, split-foyers, two-stories, condos, and larger lots inside one district without being forced into one narrow price band. Mehlville, Concord, and Lemay can offer more affordable entry points than Oakville. Resale tends to be strongest when the home has good condition, a clean layout, and a location buyers already recognize.

NeighborhoodsWhere buyers look inside this district

Oakville has more of the district’s larger, newer subdivisions and higher overall prices near parks, highways, and the Mississippi corridor. Mehlville is practical and varied, with older ranches, split-levels, and buyers looking for affordability inside a known South County district. Concord has a central South County feel and can overlap with stronger surrounding demand depending on the exact street and boundary. Lemay is more affordable and mixed, attracting buyers who want access and price more than polish. Bayless pockets can provide entry-level opportunities, but buyers need to watch condition and district boundaries carefully. Fenton portions can offer more space and a suburban feel, but location details matter because Fenton crosses multiple districts. Arnold-area overlap gives some buyers a Jefferson County-style price conversation while still looking around the South County market.

2026 sales dataOakville High vs. Mehlville High: Does the Same Ranch Really Cost More on the Oakville Side?

At first glance, yes. Similar ranches in the Oakville High area often sold for more than those in the Mehlville High area.

But when I controlled for the age of the house, most of that price gap disappeared. And in these comparisons, Mehlville High-area ranches generally sold faster.

Start with the big picture.

Across all 285 Mehlville School District sales in this dataset, the 137 homes listed in MLS as Oakville High had a median sale price of $385,000. The 148 listed as Mehlville High had a median of $323,500.

That’s a $61,500 difference.

But those weren’t the same houses.

The median Oakville High-area home was 2,288 square feet and built in 1979. The median Mehlville High-area home was 1,766 square feet and built in 1966.

Their price per square foot was almost identical: $174 in the Oakville High area and $177 in the Mehlville High area.

So I narrowed the comparison.

I looked only at 3-bedroom, 2-bath ranches or bungalows, then separated them into 300-square-foot size bands.

The Oakville High side still came out higher in all four groups. The differences ranged from $11,900 to $69,000.

But something else kept showing up.

The Oakville High-area houses were generally newer.

In the 1,200–1,499-square-foot group, for example, the 7 Oakville High-area sales had a $332,000 median and were built around 1975. The 12 Mehlville High-area sales had a $273,500 median and were built around 1960.

That’s a $58,500 gap—but also about 15 years of difference in the houses being compared.

First read: Oakville High-area ranches sold for more in all four size groups—but the Oakville homes were generally newer, so size alone doesn’t make these houses truly comparable.

So I ran it again and controlled for age.

Among 3-bedroom, 2-bath ranches from 1,200–2,099 square feet built from 1950–1964, the 3 Oakville High-area sales had a $280,500 median. The 21 Mehlville High-area sales had a $274,000 median.

The gap fell to $6,500.

For homes built from 1965–1979, the 14 Oakville High-area sales had a $332,500 median compared with $322,500 among 6 Mehlville High-area sales.

Now the difference was $10,000.

And the Mehlville High-area homes in that age group actually sold for more per square foot: $200 versus $190.

That’s why I wouldn’t look at the original $61,500 overall difference and conclude that being on the Oakville High side explains it.

A lot of what looks like a high-school-area price difference is really a different-house problem.

What this means if you’re choosing between the two

  • Don’t assume you’re paying more because of the high-school area. If an Oakville High-area ranch costs more, first look at whether you’re actually comparing a newer house with an older one.
  • Compare the house before comparing the price. Age, size, condition, updates, basement, lot and subdivision can matter more than the high-school label attached to the listing.
  • If you’re shopping older ranches, the price difference may be much smaller than the overall medians suggest. The same-age comparisons narrowed the gap substantially.
  • Mehlville High-area ranches moved faster in these historical groups. The 1950–64 and 1965–79 groups had a 4-day median days on market on the Mehlville side, compared with 6 and 14.5 days on the Oakville side. That doesn’t predict the next sale, but it’s worth knowing when you’re shopping.

What this data can’t tell you

I wouldn’t turn this into a rule that two similar houses should be within $6,500 or $10,000 of each other. These are small groups—some have only 2, 3 or 4 sales. The 900–1,199-square-foot group is especially worth noting: it had just 4 sales on each side with fairly similar ages, and Oakville was still $52,500 higher. That’s too little data for me to tell you why.

Age isn’t the only thing I haven’t controlled for, either. Lot size, updates, condition, basements and the specific subdivision can all change the price. These are closed sales from January 1 through October 5, 2026, not today’s inventory. And the high-school areas here are based on the school listed in MLS. If the school assignment matters to your purchase, verify the exact address directly with Mehlville School District.

Questions buyers ask

Are houses in the Oakville High area more expensive than the Mehlville High area?

Overall, they were in this 2026 dataset. The 137 sales listed as Oakville High had a $385,000 median, compared with $323,500 among 148 sales listed as Mehlville High. But the Oakville High-area homes were also larger and newer. When I compared 3-bedroom, 2-bath ranches from the same eras, the median gap narrowed to $6,500 for homes built from 1950–1964 and $10,000 for homes built from 1965–1979. The high-school assignment is based on MLS and should be verified by exact address.

Do homes near Mehlville High sell faster than homes near Oakville High?

They did in several of these historical ranch comparisons. For 3-bedroom, 2-bath ranches built from 1950–1964, the median days on market was 4 days in the Mehlville High area versus 6 days in the Oakville High area. For homes built from 1965–1979, it was 4 days versus 14.5 days. Those are historical results from small groups of sales, not a prediction of how quickly the next house will sell.

Honest takeWhat to know before you decide

Mehlville is one of the better value plays in South County because it gives buyers options. It does not have the prestige pricing of Lindbergh, but that is exactly why many buyers can still get a livable house here. The weak spot is inconsistency; some homes feel like great long-term buys, while others need too much work for the price. If you want South County function, school stability, and room in the budget, Mehlville is hard to ignore.
