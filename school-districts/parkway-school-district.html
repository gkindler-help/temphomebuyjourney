Who it's forBuyer fit for Parkway School District

Parkway is for buyers who want elite-school access with a wide range of West County and central-corridor lifestyles. It fits move-up buyers, relocation buyers, and families who want strong academics, established neighborhoods, and enough inventory variety to compare options.

What you're paying forValue breakdown

You are paying for scale, reputation, and location. Parkway is a massive district with multiple high schools, and that means the buyer experience can vary a lot depending on which attendance area you are actually in. The premium buys access to respected schools, mature suburbs, stronger resale, and proximity to major employment corridors. The risk is assuming all Parkway homes are equal, because the neighborhood, high school boundary, commute, and price tier all change the market.

NeighborhoodsWhere buyers look inside this district

Chesterfield offers larger homes, subdivisions, and upper-middle to luxury price points with strong family demand. Creve Coeur has a more central feel, with medical, corporate, and commuter convenience driving prices. Town and Country is higher-end and quieter, attracting buyers who want larger lots, privacy, and prestige. Clarkson Valley is more secluded and estate-like, with buyers paying for space and exclusivity. Des Peres is convenient and retail-heavy nearby, with strong demand from buyers who want location and schools. Manchester is a more accessible Parkway entry point, with practical homes and strong everyday convenience. Maryland Heights gives buyers a more affordable way into parts of Parkway, but the housing and neighborhood feel can vary. Olivette has central-corridor appeal, with buyers drawn to location, commute, and long-term resale more than big lots.

High schoolsThe four Parkway high schools: which one does a home feed into?

Parkway School District is large enough that saying a home is "in Parkway" only tells you part of the story. For buyers with kids approaching high school, the next question is usually: which Parkway high school?

Parkway has four comprehensive high schools — Parkway Central, Parkway North, Parkway West and Parkway South — plus Fern Ridge, the district's alternative high school. The four comprehensive schools serve different portions of the district:

  • Parkway Central High School is in Chesterfield on North Woods Mill Road. Established in 1954, Central is the original Parkway high school.
  • Parkway North High School is on Fee Fee Road and serves portions of the northern Parkway area around Creve Coeur and nearby communities.
  • Parkway West High School is on Clayton Road in the Ballwin/Chesterfield area and serves portions of the western and southwestern part of the district.
  • Parkway South High School is on Hanna Road in Manchester and serves portions of the southern Parkway area.

The important word there is portions. I wouldn't try to determine a Parkway high school based on the city name alone.

Parkway serves eight municipalities in western St. Louis County, and school attendance boundaries do not simply follow municipal lines. Two homes with the same city or postal address can potentially have different school assignments depending on where they sit within the district's attendance boundaries.

This is especially important when searching for homes in Parkway School District because buyers sometimes see "Chesterfield," "Ballwin" or "Creve Coeur" and assume they know which schools come with the address. That's not a safe assumption.

Boundaries can also change through redistricting. If attending a particular Parkway high school matters to your purchase, use Parkway School District's official school and boundary locator for the exact property address. I'd verify it again before making a home-buying decision based on that assignment rather than relying solely on the schools automatically displayed on a real estate listing.

PerformanceHow does Parkway School District perform?

Parkway has a long-standing reputation as one of the stronger public-school districts in West St. Louis County, and the district reports that its comprehensive high schools rank among the top 10% of high schools in Missouri.

That's useful context, but I wouldn't stop there.

If you're researching the Parkway School District rating, start with Missouri DESE — the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education. That's where you can review official state-reported information rather than relying on a number from a real estate website or an article that may be several years old. Under the state's current MSIP 6 reporting, Parkway scored in the mid-80s on its baseline Annual Performance Report; confirm the district's current APR score directly with DESE, since these figures update annually and older MSIP 5 scores are not comparable.

GreatSchools can also be useful for researching individual Parkway schools and comparing information in one place. Just remember that a GreatSchools rating is a starting point, not my definition of whether a school is "good." Look at the underlying information and decide which factors actually matter to your family.

The same applies to district-wide performance. Parkway has roughly 18,000 students across about 28 schools, so a district average can't tell you everything about an individual elementary, middle or high school.

That matters when buyers start comparing Parkway vs. Rockwood schools. Both are large West County districts, but I wouldn't choose between two homes based only on which district has the better overall number in a particular year. I'd compare the specific schools tied to each address, the programs important to your family, the homes you can actually afford and the location that works for your daily life.

If you're still deciding where in West County to focus, start with my West County guide and your actual buying power. School district preference matters, but so do the house, payment, commute and neighborhood you get for the money.

FAQParkway School District: common buyer questions

How many high schools does Parkway School District have?

Parkway has four comprehensive high schools: Parkway Central, Parkway North, Parkway West and Parkway South. The district also operates Fern Ridge as an alternative high school.

Which Parkway high school will my home feed into?

It depends on the exact property address. Parkway School District boundaries do not simply follow city lines, so don't assume a Chesterfield, Ballwin or Creve Coeur address automatically tells you the high school. Use Parkway's official school locator to verify the current assignment for a specific home.

Is Parkway School District a good district?

Parkway is generally regarded as a high-performing West St. Louis County district, and Parkway reports that its comprehensive high schools rank among the top 10% in Missouri. For current performance data, I recommend checking Missouri DESE and then researching the individual schools that would actually serve the home rather than relying on the district reputation alone.

Parkway vs. Rockwood — which is better for buyers?

I wouldn't give every buyer the same answer. Both are large West County districts, and the better choice depends on the specific schools, programs, home prices, commute and neighborhoods that fit your family. If you're comparing homes in Parkway and Rockwood, research the exact school assignments for both addresses rather than making the decision from district-wide ratings alone.

Honest takeWhat to know before you decide

Parkway deserves its elite reputation, but it is not a simple one-size-fits-all district. Its strength is choice: price points, neighborhoods, commute patterns, and school options. Its weakness is complexity, because buyers can overpay if they lean on the Parkway name without understanding the exact pocket. If you want West County schools with more flexibility than Clayton or Ladue, Parkway gives you a lot to work with.

Property taxes · 2026What are property taxes in the Parkway School District?

Updated October 2026 · MLS sales records

The median annual property tax on homes that sold in the Parkway School District in 2026 was $4,763, according to MLS sales records. The middle half of tax bills ran from $3,862 to $6,308, across 398 sales from January 1 to October 5, 2026.

The bill follows the county’s assessed value and the local tax rates, not the price the house sold for, so two similar homes on the same street can have different bills.
