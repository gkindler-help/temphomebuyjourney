University City School District: What Home Buyers Need to Know

Last updated September 6, 2026

The University City School District, officially the School District of University City, is a smaller mid-county school district serving roughly 2,550 students across about seven schools. Its location is one of the first things that makes it different from many of the districts buyers compare around St. Louis County.

University City sits directly next to the City of St. Louis and near Clayton and Washington University. That gives buyers a different housing and location equation than they get farther west in districts such as Parkway or Rockwood.

The district has four K-5 elementary schools — Barbara C. Jordan, Flynn Park, Jackson Park and Pershing — followed by Brittany Woods Middle School and one comprehensive high school, University City High School. That single-high-school structure makes one part of the school search relatively simple. If an address is confirmed inside the School District of University City, University City High is the district's comprehensive high school.

The district is currently state-accredited by Missouri DESE. As with every district page I write, I recommend checking the latest DESE information when school performance or accreditation is an important part of your purchase.

Who it's forWho is University City School District a good fit for?

University City tends to come into the conversation for buyers who want to stay relatively close to the center of the St. Louis metro while still buying in St. Louis County. That's an important distinction.

University City is an inner-ring community adjacent to the independent City of St. Louis. It is also near Clayton and Washington University, so buyers considering U City are often making a different location decision than someone looking farther into West County or St. Charles County. I would start with that location question before getting too focused on a school-district rating. Where do you work? Where do you spend your time? How much house do you need? How important is being closer to the central part of the metro compared with having newer housing or a different amount of space?

The School District of University City is relatively small, and it has one comprehensive high school. For some buyers, that makes the district easier to research than a large multi-high-school system where two homes in the same municipality can eventually feed different high schools. That doesn't mean every U City home has the same elementary assignment or that you can skip verifying the address. It simply means the high-school path is more straightforward once you know the property is actually inside the district.

What you're paying forWhat are you paying for when you buy in University City?

University City is a good example of why I don't like reducing a home search to "Which school district is best?" You're buying much more than a district name. You're buying a house, a specific location, a commute, a lot, a floor plan and a monthly payment. In an established inner-ring community such as University City, you're also looking at housing stock that can be very different from what you'll find in newer parts of the metro.

Instead of treating older or newer as automatically better, I would look at what the individual property gives you. How much finished space are you getting? Does it have the number of bedrooms you need? Is there a garage? What is the basement like? What updates have already been completed, and what major systems may need attention after you buy? Condition matters because two homes with similar asking prices can have very different real costs.

A property that has already had significant updates may justify a different price than one where you're budgeting for the roof, HVAC, electrical work, windows or major interior renovations. I want buyers looking at that complete picture rather than assuming the list price tells them everything. Location also has value, but that value is personal. A buyer who regularly needs to reach Clayton, Washington University or the City of St. Louis may look at University City's location differently than someone whose daily routine takes them farther west.

This is where realistic buying power becomes useful. Once you know the monthly payment you're comfortable carrying, you can compare what that budget buys in University City with what it buys in another St. Louis County district and see the actual tradeoffs.

High schoolUniversity City High School and the single-high-school structure

The School District of University City has one comprehensive high school: University City High School. University City High School was established in 1930 and serves roughly 811 students. The building itself also has a distinctive piece of local history: it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1985 as part of the University City Education District.

For buyers, though, the most practical fact is the single-high-school structure. If you confirm that a property is inside the School District of University City, University City High is the district's comprehensive high school. You don't have to determine which of several district high schools serves the address. Compare that with Parkway, Rockwood or Fort Zumwalt, where confirming the district is only the first part of the school research. Those districts have multiple comprehensive high schools with different attendance areas. University City removes that particular layer of complexity.

Families with younger children still need to verify elementary assignments. The district's four K-5 elementary schools are Barbara C. Jordan, Flynn Park, Jackson Park and Pershing, followed by Brittany Woods Middle School. The school a portal lists is a fine starting reference, but I wouldn't make it the deciding fact. If the elementary assignment could change whether a University City home works for your family, confirm it through the district rather than the listing before you get attached.

PerformanceHow does University City School District perform?

The School District of University City is currently state-accredited by the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Missouri DESE). Under Missouri's current system, the district holds full accreditation. For the latest district performance data, review Missouri DESE's current report for the district before relying on a specific APR number in a home-buying decision.

I wouldn't fill that blank with an older score just to have a number on the page. Missouri's accountability system has changed over time, and school-performance information can change from one reporting cycle to the next. If the current APR matters to your purchase, Missouri DESE should be the authoritative source.

I would also separate district accreditation from individual-school research. Full accreditation is an important district-level fact. It doesn't mean every school has identical results, programs or strengths. The same is true in any district. Start with Missouri DESE for official information. GreatSchools can be useful as a secondary research source when you're looking at an individual school, but I wouldn't make a home-buying decision from one GreatSchools number.

Look at the underlying information. Research the specific elementary school connected to the property, Brittany Woods Middle School and University City High. If a particular course, program or service matters to your household, confirm that directly with the district. That's more useful than trying to turn an entire school system into one rating.

BoundariesUniversity City School District boundaries

The School District of University City includes all of University City along with small portions of Overland, Vinita Park and Wellston. That makes University City somewhat simpler than districts that divide a single municipality among several high schools, but buyers should still pay attention around the district's edges.

School-district boundaries, municipal boundaries, mailing addresses and ZIP codes are not the same thing. If you're looking at a home clearly within University City, the School District of University City is the district serving the municipality. But once you're comparing properties near neighboring communities, don't assume that being close to University City means the property is inside the district. The reverse is also true. Small portions of Overland, Vinita Park and Wellston are included within the district.

For a serious property, I would use a simple process:

  • Confirm the exact address is inside the School District of University City.
  • Verify the assigned elementary school through official district information.
  • Confirm Brittany Woods Middle School and University City High as applicable to the address.
  • Review current Missouri DESE information for the district and individual schools.
  • Use GreatSchools as additional research rather than the final authority.
  • Confirm any specific programs directly with the district if they affect your buying decision.

I recommend doing that before you get too far into negotiations if the school assignment is an important reason you're considering the house.

Honest takeMy honest take on buying in University City School District

University City is one of the districts where I think the location and the housing stock deserve just as much attention as the district-level school information. It's a smaller, fully accredited district with one comprehensive high school. It's also positioned differently within the metro than the larger suburban districts farther west. That creates a different set of tradeoffs.

If being near the City of St. Louis, Clayton or Washington University matters to your daily life, University City's location may carry a lot of weight. If your priority is newer construction, a particular subdivision layout or simply getting farther west, you may end up comparing U City with very different housing options. I wouldn't tell a buyer which of those priorities should win. I would compare actual houses.

Look at the purchase price. Look at the monthly payment. Look at the condition and the amount of work the property may need. Verify the elementary assignment and research the individual schools. Check the latest DESE information. Then decide whether that combination works for you. If you're weighing nearby mid-county options, Clayton and Ladue are natural districts to compare against.

That's a far better way to buy a home than chasing a school rating without weighing what you give up to get it. University City gives buyers a relatively straightforward district structure: four elementary schools, one middle school and one comprehensive high school. It is currently fully accredited, and its inner-ring location puts buyers close to a central part of the metro while still being in St. Louis County. Those are useful facts. The next step is figuring out how the specific house, specific school assignment and specific payment fit your priorities.

Property taxes · 2026What are property taxes in the University City School District?

The median annual property tax on homes that sold in the University City School District in 2026 was $5,021, according to MLS sales records. The middle half of tax bills ran from $2,363 to $7,106, across 87 sales from January 1 to October 5, 2026.

The bill follows the county’s assessed value and the local tax rates, not the price the house sold for, so two similar homes on the same street can have different bills.
