Ritenour School District: What Home Buyers Need to Know

Ritenour School District is one of St. Louis County's older school districts and covers a group of inner-ring communities just northwest of the City of St. Louis. The district serves roughly 6,400 students across about 10 schools, including six elementary schools, two middle schools, one comprehensive high school and early childhood programming.

For buyers, Ritenour is relatively simple to understand at the high-school level. It is a single-high-school district, so once you've confirmed that a property is actually inside Ritenour School District, Ritenour High School is the district's comprehensive high school. The more complicated part is determining whether the home is actually inside Ritenour in the first place.

The district covers all or portions of several municipalities, including Overland, Breckenridge Hills, St. John, St. Ann, Woodson Terrace and Bridgeton. Like most of St. Louis County, municipal boundaries and school-district boundaries don't always line up.

Ritenour is also currently fully accredited by Missouri DESE. There is some important context behind its recent state performance score, which I'll explain below, because looking at the APR percentage without understanding Missouri's current accreditation process could give buyers the wrong impression.

High schoolRitenour High School and the single-high-school structure

Ritenour School District has one comprehensive high school: Ritenour High School at 9100 St. Charles Rock Road in Breckenridge Hills. Ritenour High was established in 1911 and serves roughly 2,041 students.

From a home-buying perspective, I like the simplicity of a single-high-school district. Compare that with Hazelwood, Parkway or Rockwood. In those districts, confirming that a home is in the district is only step one. You also need to determine which of several high schools serves that address. Ritenour doesn't have that extra high-school feeder question. Once an address is confirmed inside the district, Ritenour High is the comprehensive high school.

You should still verify elementary and middle-school assignments for a specific property. Ritenour operates six elementary schools and two middle schools, and those attendance boundaries can matter to families with younger children. I wouldn't rely exclusively on the school information automatically shown on Zillow, Realtor.com, the MLS or another real estate website. Those tools are useful for the initial search, but if school assignment affects whether you'd buy the property, verify the exact address through Ritenour's official district information.

AccreditationRitenour accreditation and school performance

Ritenour School District is currently fully accredited by the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Missouri DESE). That's the correct current classification, but Ritenour's recent Annual Performance Report requires some explanation.

For the 2024-25 reporting period, Ritenour's APR score was approximately 67%, down from roughly 71% the previous year. Under Missouri's MSIP 6 framework, a score in that range would normally fall within the range associated with provisional accreditation. However, Missouri DESE has delayed accreditation-status changes until 2027. That means Ritenour remains fully accredited today.

I wouldn't describe the district as provisionally accredited based solely on the APR number because that is not its current state classification. At the same time, I don't think buyers are helped by hiding the performance number, because the context is important. The accurate way to look at it is: Ritenour is currently fully accredited, its 2024-25 APR was about 67%, and Missouri has paused accreditation classification changes until 2027. Because 2027 could bring changes to how districts are classified, I recommend checking Missouri DESE directly if accreditation is an important part of your home-buying decision. A district page written today shouldn't be treated as a permanent substitute for current state information.

Ritenour is also participating in Missouri's Success-Ready Student Network and the state's School Innovation Waiver. It is one of roughly 20 districts working on alternative student-assessment methods alongside the state's traditional reporting system. That's worth understanding because education performance isn't necessarily being evaluated through one single measure.

I would still use Missouri DESE as the official source for state accountability data. GreatSchools can also be useful for looking at individual school profiles, but I wouldn't make a home-buying decision from the headline GreatSchools number alone. Look at the underlying information, individual schools and programs that matter to your family. A district-level APR is useful context, but it isn't a complete description of every school or every student's experience.

HistoryRitenour's history in St. Louis County

Ritenour also has a long history in the St. Louis region. The district was founded in 1846, making it one of the older school systems in the area. In 1867, Ritenour became the first school district in the St. Louis metropolitan area to serve African American students.

That's a notable part of the district's history and one reason Ritenour's story stretches much farther back than the development of many of the suburban districts buyers commonly compare today. I wouldn't use history as a substitute for researching the district as it operates now, but I do think it's useful context when you're trying to understand a school system that's been part of the region for more than 175 years.

CommunitiesWhat communities are in Ritenour School District?

Ritenour School District covers a collection of municipalities in inner-ring northwest St. Louis County. The district includes:

  • All of Breckenridge Hills
  • All of Edmundson
  • All of Overland
  • All of Sycamore Hills
  • All of Woodson Terrace
  • Most of Charlack
  • Most of St. John
  • Much of St. Ann
  • Much of Vinita Park
  • Portions of Bridgeton

For buyers, that list is useful context but shouldn't replace checking a specific address. St. Louis County school districts don't simply follow municipal lines. A home can have a St. Ann or Bridgeton mailing address without that city name telling you which district serves it. Bridgeton is a good example because different portions of the community can fall into different school districts. The same general issue occurs throughout St. Louis County wherever district and municipal boundaries overlap.

If Ritenour is important to your purchase, I'd verify each serious property independently. My process is straightforward:

  • Confirm that the address is actually inside Ritenour School District.
  • Verify the assigned elementary and middle school if those matter to your family.
  • Confirm Ritenour High as the comprehensive high-school path.
  • Review the latest Missouri DESE performance and accreditation information.
  • Research the individual schools through DESE, GreatSchools and the district itself.

That prevents a common home-search mistake: assuming a school district from the city name rather than verifying the actual property.
