Ferguson-Florissant School District: What Home Buyers Need to Know

Last updated October 7, 2026

Ferguson-Florissant School District is a large North St. Louis County district, and for buyers the first thing to understand is that it covers a wide area with multiple high schools. FFSD serves roughly 9,300 to 10,000+ students across about 23 schools and includes all or part of 11 municipalities, including Ferguson, Florissant, Berkeley, Cool Valley, Kinloch and portions of Hazelwood.

That size matters because "Ferguson-Florissant School District" does not automatically tell you which high school a particular home feeds into. The district includes McCluer High, McCluer North High and McCluer South-Berkeley / STEAM Academy, so buyers need to verify the exact address rather than assuming based on the city name in the listing.

Another important point is accreditation. Ferguson-Florissant is fully accredited by Missouri DESE. That is current. The district spent many years under provisional accreditation, but that is no longer its current status. If you find older information online saying the district is provisional, you are looking at outdated information.

For buyers, I think FFSD is best evaluated the same way I approach any large St. Louis County district: look at the actual schools tied to the address, current performance data, the programs that matter to your family and what the housing budget buys in the communities you're considering.

Who it's forWho is Ferguson-Florissant School District a good fit for?

FFSD can make sense for buyers who want to keep their search in North St. Louis County while comparing a broad range of housing options and school programs. I don't think school-district shopping works well when buyers start by chasing a reputation or a rating and then try to force everything else around it. A home purchase is more complicated than that. You still have to consider the house itself, the monthly payment, lot size, age and condition, commute, location and the specific school assignment.

Because Ferguson-Florissant covers such a large footprint, you'll see different kinds of housing depending on where you're looking. A buyer searching in Ferguson may be comparing different homes and price points than someone looking in Florissant or Berkeley, even though the properties may all fall inside the same school district. That can be useful when you're trying to find the best overall fit for a budget rather than assuming you have to buy in one narrow pocket of the metro.

FFSD also offers more than the traditional neighborhood-school structure. The district has STEAM-focused programs and a gifted program, and its STEAM Academy Middle School was named a 2025 Missouri Gold Star School. If one of those programs is especially important to your family, I would research the program directly rather than assuming district-wide availability means every student or every school has the same access.

What you're paying forWhat are you paying for when you buy in Ferguson-Florissant?

North County is one of the places where buyers can sometimes find different housing options at a given price point than they see in more expensive portions of St. Louis County. The useful comparison is not "which area is better?" It's "what does my budget buy here compared with somewhere else?" Take the houses themselves and put them side by side.

How many bedrooms are you getting? Is there a basement? Garage? Larger lot? Does one house need substantial updating while another is already in the condition you want? What does the monthly payment look like after taxes and insurance? Those questions are more useful than looking at a district rating in isolation.

If your budget buys a house in Ferguson-Florissant that works significantly better for your family than the alternatives you're seeing elsewhere, that's information worth considering. If another district has a specific school or academic program that matters enough to justify paying more for less house, that's also worth considering. I like to start with realistic buying power and work from there.

Once we know roughly what payment you're comfortable with, we can look at the actual homes available across different school districts. That's when you can make a meaningful decision about whether additional square footage, location, a particular school assignment or another factor is worth more to you. High schoolsThe Ferguson-Florissant high schools

Ferguson-Florissant is a multi-high-school district, so buyers need to pay attention to the specific attendance assignment for each home.

  • McCluer High School is located on South New Florissant Road in Florissant and was established in 1957.
  • McCluer North High School is located on Waterford Drive in Florissant and was established in 1971.
  • McCluer South-Berkeley / STEAM Academy is located in Ferguson and opened in 2004.

I would not try to determine which high school a property feeds into based only on whether the address says Ferguson, Florissant, Berkeley or Hazelwood. School district and attendance boundaries don't simply follow municipal boundaries in St. Louis County. Different sections of the same municipality may have different school assignments, and the boundaries themselves can change over time.

If a particular FFSD high school matters to your purchase, use the district's official school or boundary locator for the exact property address. If the high-school assignment is important enough that it would change whether you buy the home, verify it directly with the district before making that decision. Treat the school listed on a real estate portal as a hint, not an answer. Those sites pull from data that can lag behind FFSD's actual attendance areas, so for a home you're serious about, confirm the assignment with the district before it factors into your offer.

PerformanceHow does Ferguson-Florissant School District perform?

Ferguson-Florissant School District is fully accredited by the Missouri Department of Elementary and Secondary Education (Missouri DESE).

That point deserves to be stated clearly because the district has a long accreditation history and old information still shows up in search results. FFSD previously spent roughly 15 years under provisional accreditation and has since regained full accreditation. For someone researching "is Ferguson-Florissant accredited?" the current answer is yes.

For the latest district performance data, review Missouri DESE's current report for the district before relying on a specific APR number in a home-buying decision. I would not use an older APR number in place of the current one. Missouri's accountability system changes over time, and old percentages can create a misleading comparison if you don't know which system produced them.

There are also some current positive indicators worth looking at beyond accreditation. The district's STEAM Academy Middle School was named a 2025 Missouri Gold Star School, a state recognition aligned with the National Blue Ribbon Schools program and intended to recognize schools demonstrating high achievement or strong performance while serving disadvantaged students. FFSD also operates STEAM-focused programming and a gifted program. If those opportunities matter to your family, I would look at the current district information and program requirements rather than assuming they work the same way at every school.

GreatSchools can be useful for reviewing individual school profiles and organizing information, but I wouldn't make a home-buying decision from the headline GreatSchools rating alone. Look at what goes into the rating and compare it with Missouri DESE's official data. That distinction matters because district-level performance and individual-school performance are not the same thing. In a district with around 23 schools, the overall district number can only tell you so much about the school tied to a particular home.

CommunitiesWhat communities are in Ferguson-Florissant School District?

FFSD covers all or portions of 11 municipalities across North St. Louis County. That includes Ferguson, Florissant, Berkeley, Cool Valley, Kinloch and portions of Hazelwood, along with additional communities within the district's broader footprint.

Because the district stretches across multiple municipalities, the city listed in the property address is not enough to confirm the school district. This is a common issue throughout St. Louis County. Municipal boundaries, ZIP codes, mailing addresses and school-district boundaries are different systems, and they don't always line up neatly.

A Florissant address, for example, should not automatically be assumed to fall inside Ferguson-Florissant. Portions of the Florissant area may be served by another district, including Hazelwood. The same kind of boundary issue can occur around other municipalities at the district's edges.

If you're specifically looking for homes in Ferguson-Florissant School District, I would verify each serious property individually. Here's the sequence I'd run for any FFSD home worth an offer:

  • Confirm that the property is actually inside Ferguson-Florissant School District.
  • Use FFSD's official locator to verify the assigned schools for the exact address.
  • Research the elementary, middle and high school individually.
  • Review current Missouri DESE information for official district and school performance.
  • Use GreatSchools as an additional research tool rather than the only source.
  • Verify STEAM, gifted or other specific program requirements directly with the district if those programs matter to your decision.

It's a little more work than simply reading the school name attached to a listing, but it's worth doing before you make a major purchase based on an assumption about the schools.

Updated October 2026 · MLS sales records
