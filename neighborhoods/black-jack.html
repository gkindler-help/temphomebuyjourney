Black Jack Home Buying Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Updated October 2026 · MLS sales records. 16 closed sales inside Black Jack city limits.

How much do houses cost in Black Jack, Missouri?

In 16 sales inside Black Jack city limits that closed between January 14 and September 17, 2026, the median sale price was $235,500. The middle half sold between $207,750 and $271,588, and the full range ran from $155,000 to $338,000.

Can I buy a house in Black Jack for $250,000?

11 of 16 sales (68.8%) closed at $250,000 or less. Of those 11, 11 had three or more bedrooms and 5 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,728 sq ft, built in 1972.

How fast do homes sell in Black Jack?

The median was 8.5 days on market (CDOM), with the middle half between 4 and 28 days. 4 of the 16 sales took 30 days or more.

Do homes in Black Jack sell over asking price?

Some do, but most did not. Of the 16 sales, 7 closed above the original list price, 1 at it and 8 below it. The median sale was 98.2% of the original asking price.

How old are most homes in Black Jack?

The median house was built in 1972 and had 1,851.5 sq ft. By year built, the 16 sales broke down this way: 0 before 1950, 3 from 1950 to 1969, 13 from 1970 to 1999 and 0 in 2000 or later. The median home had 3 bedrooms.

How much does a 3-bedroom house cost in Black Jack?

In 2026, the 9 three-bedroom homes that sold inside Black Jack city limits had a median price of $225,000; the middle half sold between $201,000 and $242,000. The median home had 1,728 sq ft, built in 1986.

How much does a 4-bedroom house cost in Black Jack?

In 2026, the 7 homes with four or more bedrooms that sold inside Black Jack city limits had a median price of $269,450, with prices from $197,000 to $338,000. The median home had 1,952 sq ft, built in 1971.

How much does a ranch house cost in Black Jack?

In 2026, the 6 ranch-style homes that sold inside Black Jack city limits had a median price of $241,500, with prices from $155,000 to $307,500. The median home had 3 bedrooms and 1,852 sq ft, built in 1972.

How much does a house with 2 bathrooms cost in Black Jack?

In 2026, the 16 homes with two or more bathrooms that sold inside Black Jack city limits had a median price of $235,500; the middle half sold between $207,750 and $271,588. The median home had 3 bedrooms and 1,852 sq ft, built in 1972.

Which school district is Black Jack in?

All 16 sales inside Black Jack city limits were in the Hazelwood School District. District lines don’t always follow city limits, so verify the assigned schools for the exact address before you write an offer.

How much are property taxes in Black Jack?

In 2026 sales inside Black Jack city limits, the median annual property tax was $3,409, about 1.41% of the sale price. The tax on a listing is the seller’s most recent bill, so use this median to compare areas, then confirm the exact address with the St. Louis County Collector of Revenue before you make an offer.

Schools in Black Jack, Missouri

Black Jack is served by the Hazelwood School District. School district assignment in this area affects both buyer demand and long-term resale. Buyers with school-age children — or with future resale in mind — should verify which district serves the specific property address before committing. The Honest Take

What Buyers Actually Need to Understand

Black Jack works best for buyers who understand what it is. It is a larger North County municipality that often attracts buyers who want more subdivision-style housing than the inner ring. That matters because people who shop this market usually are not buying a headline; they are buying fit, payment, and the way the location works Monday through Friday.

In North County, the strongest pages are the honest ones. Black Jack tends to reward buyers who want established housing and practical value instead of prestige branding. The right buy here is usually about condition, block, and commute pattern more than trend.

Location & Identity

Why the Map Matters Here

Black Jack sits in far north county, which means buyers usually evaluate it through the lens of access, block-to-block feel, and nearby service corridors. That is why comparing it only by city name misses the point. In this part of the metro, the micro-location often matters as much as the municipality itself.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

What buyers actually find in Black Jack is mid-century and late-20th-century neighborhoods with more suburban spacing. The market is usually less about flash and more about whether the house solves the buyer’s real problem: payment, space, commute, or setting.

That is why Black Jack is usually best judged against Florissant, Spanish Lake, Hazelwood, not against the metro’s highest-status suburbs. Buyers who shop it on its own terms often find more house-for-the-money than they expected.
