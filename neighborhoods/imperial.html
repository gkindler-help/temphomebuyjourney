How much does a house cost in Imperial, Missouri?

The 2026 median sale price was $354,500 across 96 sales with an Imperial mailing address, according to MLS sales records. The middle half sold between $279,250 and $420,000.

Is Imperial an incorporated city?

No. Imperial is unincorporated Jefferson County, with no city government, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. County rules apply to homes with an Imperial address.

Do I need an occupancy inspection to sell a house in Imperial?

Imperial is unincorporated, and Jefferson County has no resale inspection of its own. Rock Community Fire Protection District, which serves the Imperial area, inspects existing homes at a change of ownership. The fire district for a house is on the county assessor’s parcel record. That fire-district inspection happens whether or not a buyer waives their own.

Which school district is Imperial in?

More than one. The 96 sales with an Imperial mailing address in 2026 were in Fox C-6 (57) and Windsor C-1 (39). Confirm the district for the exact address.

How much are property taxes in Imperial?

The median annual property tax on homes that sold with an Imperial mailing address in 2026 was $2,638, and the middle half of bills ran from $1,912 to $3,652, across 96 sales, according to MLS sales records. The tax on a listing is the seller’s most recent bill, so confirm the exact address with the Jefferson County Collector of Revenue.

In Imperial, does Windsor C-1 or Fox C-6 give you more house?

Windsor C-1, on 2026 sales. The 39 Imperial sales in Windsor C-1 had a $359,000 median at about 2,145 sq ft; the 57 in Fox C-6 had a $347,500 median at about 1,763 sq ft. You pay for the extra room in property tax, too: a $2,793 median bill on the Windsor side against $2,384 on the Fox side.

How much room is there to negotiate on an Imperial house?

It depends on how long the house has been listed. Imperial homes that sold within 30 days closed at a median 100.9% of the original asking price. The 33 that sat 30 days or more closed at 97.6%. That second group is where comps first, then concessions, works best.

The Honest Take

What Buyers Actually Need to Understand

Imperial works best for buyers who understand what it is. It is one of the county's strongest north-end search terms, especially for buyers who want quick access back toward South County. That matters because people who shop this market usually are not buying a headline; they are buying fit, payment, and the way the location works Monday through Friday.

In Jefferson County, buyers are usually balancing distance, land, monthly cost, and pace. Imperial fits when the buyer wants some separation from tighter county-line suburbs but still needs a location that feels workable, not remote.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

What buyers actually find in Imperial is a large mix of subdivisions and practical family-home inventory with a commuter-friendly profile. The market is usually less about flash and more about whether the house solves the buyer’s real problem: payment, space, commute, or setting.

That is why Imperial is usually best judged against Arnold, Barnhart, Oakville, not against inner-ring county suburbs with a totally different land pattern and pricing ladder. The right Jefferson County buy is often the one that gives you the most usable life for the payment.
