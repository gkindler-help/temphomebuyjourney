Patch, St. Louis.

Patch is about as far southeast as you can go and still be in St. Louis City. It runs from Robert Avenue on the north to the city limits and the River des Peres on the south, with Alabama Avenue on the west and the Mississippi River on the east. It’s the southern tip of the historic Carondelet area, roughly eight miles from downtown.

The reason to look here is straightforward: Patch is another way into South City at a relatively low entry price. The tradeoff is older housing stock and an address right at the city-county line. I wouldn’t compare a house here only with another house a few blocks away. I’d also look at what your money buys in Carondelet and what changes if you cross the River des Peres into St. Louis County.

ZIP 63111 covers Patch plus a wider section of far South City, so it is not Patch-only data. For 2025–2026, MARIS MLS data for 63111 shows a $148,000 median sale price across 38 sales, 18 median days on market, a 98.5% sale-to-list ratio, and 47.4% of sales closing at or above list. Useful context for the price range, but not a value for any specific house. In an older neighborhood, condition changes the equation quickly.

Schools

Schools in Patch

Patch is inside St. Louis City, so the public school district is St. Louis Public Schools (SLPS). I’d still verify the assigned school for the exact property rather than assuming it from the neighborhood name.

⚠ Every Patch address is in St. Louis City and served by SLPS. Verify the assigned school by exact property address before making any school-driven decision.

Buyer Tool

Fixer Upper vs. Move-In Ready

Patch has the kind of housing stock where “updated” and “renovated” don’t tell me enough. You’ll find older brick houses, small cottages, later bungalows and flats, and some Creole- and French-influenced cottages, with some housing dating back to the 1800s.

With housing this age, I look at the same systems I would anywhere in older St. Louis: sewer laterals, foundations, electrical and masonry/tuckpointing. That’s not a claim that every Patch house has those problems. A freshly painted kitchen doesn’t tell you the condition of a sewer line, and new flooring doesn’t tell you how the electrical was updated.

A cheaper house that needs several major systems can end up costing more than the house that looked expensive on day one. A buyer who understands the condition and has room in the budget may decide the lower price is worth the work. I want to know what you’re actually buying before deciding whether the price makes sense.

The Honest Take

At the Southeast Edge of St. Louis City

Patch isn’t near the county line in some abstract sense. It’s at the line. The Mississippi forms the eastern edge. The River des Peres and the city limits form the southern edge. Cross south of the river and you’re in unincorporated St. Louis County in the Lemay area.

That matters because two houses fairly close together can sit under different local governments and different tax rules. The house shouldn’t be the only thing you compare. The address matters too.

The comparison I’d make: what changes if you stay inside St. Louis City versus buying across the River des Peres in St. Louis County? In Patch, SLPS and city services apply, and city residents pay the 1% St. Louis City earnings tax. County residents across the river don’t pay that tax. That’s not an argument for either side of the river. It’s a cost that belongs in the comparison.

Put two similarly priced houses side by side, one in Patch and one just across the line, and compare more than bedrooms and square footage. What taxes apply? Which government handles the property? What does the house itself need? The border can change several of those answers in a very short distance.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

Patch isn’t a subdivision where one house tells you what the next will look like. The housing spans different eras: older brick homes and cottages, including some with Creole or French influence, alongside later bungalows and flats. That’s why I’d rather evaluate the individual property than make broad claims about the neighborhood.

At a roughly $140,000 entry point, Patch comes into the conversation for buyers who feel priced out of other parts of the metro. But a lower purchase price isn’t a guarantee of a lower total cost. A house with significant sewer, foundation, electrical or masonry work is a completely different budget than one where those systems were properly addressed. I’d rather know that before the inspection period is almost over.

FAQ

Questions Buyers Ask Before They Commit

FAQ

Where is Patch?

Patch is at the southeast tip of St. Louis City, at the southern end of the historic Carondelet area. It’s bounded by Robert Avenue (north), Alabama Avenue (west), the Mississippi River (east) and the city limits/River des Peres (south), roughly eight miles from downtown. Carondelet is directly north; the Lemay area of unincorporated St. Louis County is across the River des Peres to the south.

FAQ

Why is it called Patch?

The origin of the name isn’t well documented, and I’d rather not repeat a story as settled history. What is documented: when Carondelet was laid out, this area was part of the Carondelet Commons, land north of the River des Peres set aside for villagers to share for grazing.

FAQ

What school district serves Patch?

St. Louis Public Schools (SLPS), because Patch is inside St. Louis City. Verify the current school assignment by the property address rather than the neighborhood name or ZIP code.

FAQ

Is there a city earnings tax in Patch?

Yes. Patch is inside St. Louis City, so the 1% city earnings tax applies to residents. County residents across the River des Peres don’t pay it, so it belongs in any city-vs-county comparison.

FAQ

What should buyers watch for?

Start with the age of the house. Sewer laterals, foundations, older electrical and masonry/tuckpointing are worth investigating instead of assuming an updated interior means an updated house. That doesn’t mean a particular Patch house has those problems.

FAQ

Is Patch affordable?

It can be one of the lower-priced places to start a South City search, but affordability isn’t one number. The entry point is about $140K. MARIS MLS data for the broader 63111 ZIP shows a $148,000 median across 38 sales in 2025–2026, 18 median days on market, a 98.5% sale-to-list ratio and 47.4% selling at or above list. Those are ZIP-level numbers, not Patch-only.
