Quick answers

Oakville Home Buying Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first. Each one links to the sales behind it further down the page.

How much do houses cost in Oakville, Missouri?

In 137 closed sales listed in MLS as Oakville High area from January 1 to October 5, 2026, the median sale price was $385,000. The middle half sold between $330,000 and $480,000, and the full range ran from $205,000 to $1,025,000.

That’s a different measure from the Census median owner value (about $318,600), which reflects owners’ estimates of all homes, not recent sales.

Can I buy a house in Oakville for $300,000?

Yes, but it was a thin slice of the market: 24 of 137 sales (17.5%) closed at $300,000 or less. Of those 24, 18 had three or more bedrooms and 17 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,553 sq ft, built in 1963.

See what each budget reached →

What does $350,000 buy in Oakville?

53 of 137 sales (38.7%) closed at $350,000 or less. Of those, 46 had three or more bedrooms, 51 had two or more baths and 37 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,626 sq ft, built in 1974. Moving from $300,000 to $350,000 more than doubled the sales within reach, from 24 to 53.

What did $250,000, $400,000 and $500,000 reach in Oakville?

$250,000 or less reached 6 of 137 sales (4.4%). $400,000 or less reached 79 (57.7%), with a median of 1,816 sq ft built in 1975. $500,000 or less reached 107 (78.1%), with a median of 2,048 sq ft built in 1977.

Square-footage medians use the sales where living area was reported.

How fast do houses sell in Oakville?

The median Oakville High-area sale spent 7 days on market (CDOM). A quarter of the 137 sales took 3 days or less, but the slowest quarter took 36 days or more: 40 sales (29.2%) took 30+ days, 13 took 60+ days and the longest took 162 days.

See market time by price band →

Do Oakville homes sell over asking?

More often than not, measured against the original list price: 79 of 137 (57.7%) closed above it, 18 (13.1%) closed at it and 40 (29.2%) closed below it. The median sale was 101.25% of the original ask. It varied by price: 12 of 17 sales from $250,000 to $299,999 closed above the original ask, compared with 13 of 27 from $350,000 to $399,999.

What kind of houses are common in Oakville?

Mostly houses built from the 1960s through the 1990s. Of the 137 sales, 62 (45.3%) were built 1960–1979, 43 (31.4%) 1980–1999, 24 (17.5%) in 2000 or later and 8 (5.8%) before 1960. The median house was 2,288 sq ft and built in 1979. Traditional and Ranch were the most common MLS styles.

Are ranch homes common in Oakville?

Yes. 68 of the 137 sales (49.6%) listed a ranch style, including ranch combinations. Ranches made up most of the lower price bands and thinned out as prices rose: 76.5% of $250,000–$299,999 sales versus 11.8% of $500,000–$599,999 sales.

Is Oakville in the Mehlville School District?

Oakville is served by the Mehlville R-IX School District. In this dataset, Mehlville R-IX sales were listed in MLS under two high schools, Oakville High (137 sales) and Mehlville High (148 sales), so which one a house falls under depends on its exact address.

Mehlville School District buyer guide →

Does an Oakville address automatically attend Oakville High School?

No. All 137 Oakville High-area sales were in ZIP 63129, but 16 other sales in 63129 listed Mehlville High. Mailing city doesn’t settle it either: 83 of the 137 Oakville High-area sales listed “St Louis” as the city in MLS, and only 34 listed “Oakville.” Verify attendance for the exact address with Mehlville R-IX.

Is Oakville incorporated?

No. Oakville has no city government of its own; it’s part of unincorporated St. Louis County, so County rules generally apply. A mailing address alone doesn’t confirm that a property is unincorporated; check the St. Louis County GIS map.

Does Oakville require an occupancy inspection?

For properties in unincorporated St. Louis County, yes. The County requires an occupancy permit when a new occupant moves in after a sale or rental change, and the new occupant must wait until it’s issued. An inspection fee accompanies the application; confirm the current amount with the County.

How do Oakville High-area home prices compare with the Mehlville High area?

Higher overall: a $385,000 median across 137 sales versus $323,500 across 148. But the houses weren’t the same. The median Oakville High-area home was larger (2,288 vs. 1,766 sq ft) and newer (1979 vs. 1966), and price per square foot was nearly identical ($174 vs. $177).

Know Your Problem

Price Problem, Scarcity Problem or Competition Problem?

Buyers often assume every frustrating search is a budget problem. It usually isn’t that simple.

Price problem

The house you want exists, but it typically trades above your budget.

Scarcity problem

Very few homes matching what you want actually come to market.

Competition problem

The house appears, but buyers compete hard when it does.

Raising your budget can help with a price problem. It doesn’t automatically solve the other two. Knowing which one you have changes what you should do next.

MLS-marketed condition is part of this too. Across the cohort, 48 listings were marketed as Updated / Move-In Ready, 83 as Standard / Unclear, 5 as Needs Work / As-Is and 1 as Mixed / Partially Updated. In the $400K–$449,999 band, 8 of 14 were marketed as Updated / Move-In Ready—that doesn’t mean $400,000 guarantees an updated house.

“MLS-Marketed Condition” is a research classification based on how the listing was marketed. It is not an inspection finding. “Standard / Unclear” doesn’t mean dated or poor—it means the listing didn’t support a stronger classification.

The Honest Take

What Buyers Actually Need to Understand

Oakville is one of the clearer long-term ownership markets in South County. It is physically larger than many nearby communities, and it usually attracts buyers who are shopping for staying power: more house, more suburban spread, and a neighborhood feel that leans stable instead of transitional.

The Census Bureau puts Oakville's owner-occupied rate at 86%, almost identical to Concord's, but Oakville sits on a much larger footprint. That larger scale changes the way buyers search it: not as a single pocket, but as a broad South County market with distinct subareas and lot patterns.

Home Rules

Before You Close on an Oakville House: Occupancy and Home Rules

Oakville is an unincorporated community in St. Louis County, not a city with its own municipal government. An Oakville mailing address does not by itself confirm that a property is unincorporated. County rules below apply when the property is within unincorporated St. Louis County. Check the St. Louis County GIS map or contact the County before relying on them.

Do I need an occupancy permit when I buy or rent a home in Oakville?

If the property is in unincorporated St. Louis County, the County requires an occupancy permit when a new occupant moves in after a sale or rental change. The County’s application covers both sale and rental situations, and the new occupant must wait until the permit is issued before occupying the home.

See the County’s single-family dwelling occupancy application and permits and applications for current instructions.

Is there a sewer lateral repair program in Oakville?

Unincorporated properties may be eligible for the St. Louis County Sewer Lateral Repair Program. Eligibility applies; assistance is not automatic. Check the County’s current program requirements and ordinance guidebook before budgeting for a repair.

Do I need a permit for a fence, deck, shed, or pool?

St. Louis County Transportation and Public Works handles applicable residential permits for unincorporated properties. The County’s ordinance guidebook describes these general rules:

  • Fence: A fence 6 feet or shorter generally does not require a building permit. Zoning, sight-distance, and private subdivision or property restrictions may still apply.
  • Deck: Permit and code review may apply, depending on the project. Confirm requirements for the specific address and work before construction.
  • Shed: A shed larger than 120 square feet requires a building permit.
  • Pool: Qualifying pools require a County permit and must meet applicable enclosure, setback, and electrical requirements.

Requirements can depend on the property and project details. Check with the County before work begins.

Are electrical, plumbing, or mechanical permits required?

County trade permits may apply to electrical, plumbing, and mechanical work. Check the County’s permits and applications for the project’s requirements.

Do Oakville landlords need a rental license or registration?

A County occupancy permit is required when a new occupant moves in after a rental change at an unincorporated property. The available County information reviewed for this guide does not establish a separate local landlord license or registration requirement. Confirm with the County whether any additional rules apply to the property or rental arrangement.

Who handles property maintenance and code concerns?

For unincorporated properties, St. Louis County Neighborhood Preservation handles applicable property maintenance and code matters. Confirm that the address is in unincorporated County territory before contacting a city department.

How much does the occupancy permit cost?

The County’s downloadable occupancy application does not publish the current inspection fee. The fee must accompany the application, so confirm the current amount in the County permitting portal or with the County before submitting.

Should I check flood risk for an Oakville address?

Yes, address by address. Oakville sits along the Mississippi and Meramec river corridors, so flood exposure isn’t uniform across the community. Look up the exact address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and get an insurance quote before you commit.

How do I confirm which schools serve the house?

Verify the assigned schools for the exact address with the Mehlville School District. An Oakville mailing address or ZIP code doesn’t settle the school assignment, and this page’s data defines Oakville by the high school listed in MLS.

Before you buy, rent, or start a project: verify the property’s jurisdiction by exact address, then confirm current requirements with St. Louis County. Rules can differ for a property inside an incorporated municipality.

Sources checked September 25, 2026: St. Louis County Transportation and Public Works occupancy and permit pages; St. Louis County Planning ordinance guidebook. Requirements are address- and project-dependent.
