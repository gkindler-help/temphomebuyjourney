Florissant Home Buying Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first. Each one links to the sales behind it further down the page.

How much do houses cost in Florissant, Missouri?

In 167 sales inside the City of Florissant’s Census boundary that closed between January 6 and October 1, 2026, the median sale price was $180,000. The middle half sold between $149,900 and $225,000, and the full range ran from $62,400 to $410,000.

Can I buy a house in Florissant for $150,000?

46 of 167 sales (27.5%) closed at $150,000 or less. Of those 46, 31 had three or more bedrooms, 20 had two or more baths and 28 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,030.5 sq ft, built in 1954.

What does $200,000 buy in Florissant?

107 of 167 sales (64.1%) closed at $200,000 or less. Of those 107, 90 had three or more bedrooms, 65 had two or more baths and 70 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,092 sq ft, built in 1956.

How fast do houses sell in Florissant?

The median sale spent 27 days on market (CDOM). A quarter of the 167 sales took 7 days or less; the slowest quarter took about 51 days or more. 78 sales took 30+ days, 36 took 60+ and the longest took 337.

Do Florissant homes sell over asking?

Measured against the original list price, 57 of 167 (34.1%) closed above it, 26 (15.6%) at it and 84 (50.3%) below it. The median sale was 99.7% of the original ask.

What kind of houses are common in Florissant?

Almost entirely houses built before 1980. Of the 167 sales, 100 (59.9%) were built before 1960, 63 (37.7%) from 1960 to 1979, 2 (1.2%) from 1980 to 1999 and 2 (1.2%) in 2000 or later. The median house was about 1,300 sq ft, built in 1958, and 107 of 167 (64.1%) listed a ranch style.

See how the house changes by band →

Does a Florissant mailing address mean the house is in the City of Florissant?

Not necessarily. The 167 sales inside the city boundary were in 63031 (117) and 63033 (50), and ZIP codes don’t follow city limits. 164 of the 167 listed “Florissant” as the city in MLS. Check the St. Louis County GIS map for the exact address.

Which school district is Florissant in?

Two. Of the 167 sales inside the city boundary, 90 were in the Ferguson-Florissant School District and 77 in the Hazelwood School District. Houses a few streets apart can be in different districts. Verify the assigned schools for the exact address before you write an offer.

Schools and district boundaries →

How much are property taxes in Florissant?

It depends on which school district side of Florissant the house is on. In 2026 sales inside the City of Florissant’s Census boundary, the median annual property tax was $2,105 on the Ferguson-Florissant side (about 1.22% of the sale price) and $2,653 on the Hazelwood side (about 1.41% of the sale price). The tax on a listing is the seller’s most recent bill, so use these medians to compare areas, then confirm the exact address with the St. Louis County Collector of Revenue before you make an offer.

How do Ferguson-Florissant-side prices compare with the Hazelwood side of Florissant?

Nearly the same: a $175,000 median across 90 sales on the Ferguson-Florissant side versus $182,000 across 77 on the Hazelwood side. The houses were similar too (1,300 vs. 1,321 sq ft), and so was price per square foot ($135 vs. $139). The difference was speed: Hazelwood-side homes had a 15-day median market time versus 29.

Is Florissant its own city?

Yes. Florissant is an incorporated city in St. Louis County with its own city government. Before you close, ask the city what it requires when a house changes hands, such as an occupancy permit or inspection.

Methodology

How This Florissant Analysis Was Built

This analysis uses 167 closed-sale records from the STL Home Journey 2026 St. Louis County sales dataset (January 6–October 1, 2026). A sale counts here when its listing’s map coordinates fall inside the Census Bureau’s boundary for the City of Florissant, using the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2024 TIGER/Line place boundary. The same records feed the St. Louis County school district housing analysis.

  • Historical closed sales are not current inventory.
  • Days on market uses CDOM.
  • Sale ÷ original ask compares the close price with the original list price.
  • Ranch share counts any MLS style that includes “Ranch.”
  • Listing coordinates can be imprecise near the boundary. Verify jurisdiction and school assignment for the exact address.

The Honest Take

What Buyers Actually Need to Understand

Florissant works best for buyers who understand what it is. It is one of North County's largest and most recognizable municipalities, with broad housing choice and strong everyday convenience. That matters because people who shop this market usually are not buying a headline; they are buying fit, payment, and the way the location works Monday through Friday.

In North County, the strongest pages are the honest ones. Florissant tends to reward buyers who want established housing and practical value instead of prestige branding. The right buy here is usually about condition, block, and commute pattern more than trend.

Location & Identity

Why the Map Matters Here

Florissant sits in north county core, which means buyers usually evaluate it through the lens of access, block-to-block feel, and nearby service corridors. That is why comparing it only by city name misses the point. In this part of the metro, the micro-location often matters as much as the municipality itself.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

What buyers actually find in Florissant is a wide mix of ranches, split-levels, condos, and later suburban subdivisions. The market is usually less about flash and more about whether the house solves the buyer’s real problem: payment, space, commute, or setting.

That is why Florissant is usually best judged against Black Jack, Hazelwood, Spanish Lake, not against the metro’s highest-status suburbs. Buyers who shop it on its own terms often find more house-for-the-money than they expected.
