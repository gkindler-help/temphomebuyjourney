Chesterfield Home Buying Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

How much do houses cost in Chesterfield, Missouri?

In 120 sales inside the City of Chesterfield’s Census boundary that closed between January 5 and September 29, 2026, the median sale price was $685,000. The middle half sold between $544,382 and $890,000, and the full range ran from $366,000 to $2,000,000.

That’s a different measure from the Census median owner value for Chesterfield (about $472,500), which reflects owners’ estimates of all homes, not recent sales.

Can I buy a house in Chesterfield for $500,000?

18 of 120 sales (15.0%) closed at $500,000 or less. Of those 18, all 18 had three or more bedrooms, all 18 had two or more baths and 8 were ranch-style. Their median was 2,502 sq ft, built in 1976.

What does $700,000 buy in Chesterfield?

64 of 120 sales (53.3%) closed at $700,000 or less. Of those 64, all 64 had three or more bedrooms, all 64 had two or more baths and 19 were ranch-style. Their median was 2,783 sq ft, built in 1977.

How fast do houses sell in Chesterfield?

The median sale spent 8 days on market (CDOM). A quarter of the 120 sales took 3 days or less; the slowest quarter took about 36 days or more. 37 sales took 30+ days, 14 took 60+ and the longest took 119.

Do Chesterfield homes sell over asking?

Measured against the original list price, 54 of 120 (45.0%) closed above it, 14 (11.7%) at it and 52 (43.3%) below it. The median sale was 100.0% of the original ask.

What kind of houses are common in Chesterfield?

Mostly houses from the 1960s through the 1990s. Of the 120 sales, 1 (0.8%) was built before 1960, 53 (44.2%) from 1960 to 1979, 53 (44.2%) from 1980 to 1999 and 13 (10.8%) in 2000 or later. The median house was about 3,326 sq ft, built in 1982, and 28 of 120 (23.3%) listed a ranch style.

Does a 63017 or 63005 address mean the house is in the City of Chesterfield?

Not necessarily. Of the 120 sales inside the city boundary, 91 were in 63017, 28 in 63005 and 1 in 63141, and ZIP codes don’t follow city limits. 119 of the 120 listed “Chesterfield” as the city in MLS. Check the St. Louis County GIS map for the exact address.

Which school district is Chesterfield in?

Two. Of the 120 sales inside the city boundary, 75 were in the Parkway School District (60 Parkway Central High, 15 Parkway West High) and 45 in the Rockwood School District (36 Marquette High, 9 Lafayette High). Verify the assigned schools for the exact address before you write an offer.

How much are property taxes in Chesterfield?

It depends on which school district side of Chesterfield the house is on. In 2026 sales inside Chesterfield, the median annual property tax was $5,778 on the Parkway side (about 0.95% of the sale price) and $9,036 on the Rockwood side (about 1.00% of the sale price). The tax on a listing is the seller’s most recent bill, so use these medians to compare areas, then confirm the exact address with the St. Louis County Collector of Revenue before you make an offer.

How do Parkway-side prices compare with the Rockwood side of Chesterfield?

The Rockwood side sold higher: an $885,000 median across 45 sales versus $620,000 across 75 on the Parkway side. The Rockwood-side homes were larger (4,006 vs. 3,034 sq ft) and newer (1989 vs. 1978), and they sold for more per square foot ($225 vs. $201). They also moved faster, a 4-day median versus 11.

Is Chesterfield its own city?

Yes. Chesterfield is an incorporated city in St. Louis County with its own city government. Before you close, ask the city what it requires when a house changes hands, such as an occupancy permit or inspection.

Methodology

How This Chesterfield Analysis Was Built

This analysis uses 120 closed-sale records from the STL Home Journey 2026 St. Louis County sales dataset (January 5–September 29, 2026). A sale counts here when its listing’s map coordinates fall inside the Census Bureau’s boundary for the City of Chesterfield, using the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2024 TIGER/Line place boundary. The same records feed the St. Louis County school district housing analysis.

  • Historical closed sales are not current inventory.
  • Days on market uses CDOM.
  • Sale ÷ original ask compares the close price with the original list price.
  • Ranch share counts any MLS style that includes “Ranch.”
  • Listing coordinates can be imprecise near the boundary. Verify jurisdiction and school assignment for the exact address.

The Honest Take

Big-Map Searching With Big-Suburb Advantages

Chesterfield is one of the metro's large suburban search zones. The city describes itself as covering about 32 square miles, and the Census Bureau lists a 2020 population of 49,999.

That scale changes the way buyers should shop it. Chesterfield is not one subdivision and not one town center. It is a major municipality where the right neighborhood, commute pattern, and house style matter just as much as the city name on the listing.

The upside is breadth. Buyers who need options — lot sizes, newer homes, established pockets, and broad service access — often find more real choice here than in smaller West County cities.

Location & Identity

A Large City With Strong Recreation Infrastructure

The city's parks system is a major part of the appeal. Central Park is described by the city as a 55-acre park with a broad set of amenities, and the city also promotes the Chesterfield Amphitheater and Family Aquatic Center as regional-quality assets.

That infrastructure reinforces Chesterfield's role as a full-service suburb rather than a single-corridor market.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

Chesterfield buyers usually want room — in the map, in the house, or in the decision-making. The city is large enough that buyers can pursue different versions of suburban life without leaving the municipal boundary.

That also means comparisons matter more than headline stats. Some buyers want newer or larger homes; others want more established neighborhoods with easier access to retail, parks, or major roads.

In short, Chesterfield rewards a narrowed search inside a broad city.
