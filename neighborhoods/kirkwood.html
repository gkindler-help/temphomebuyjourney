Quick answers

Kirkwood Home Buying Questions, Answered With 2026 Sales

Short answers first. Each one links to the sales behind it further down the page.

How much do houses cost in Kirkwood, Missouri?

In 80 sales inside the City of Kirkwood’s Census boundary that closed between January 8 and October 1, 2026, the median sale price was $566,750. The middle half sold between $388,750 and $807,500, and the full range ran from $75,000 to $2,750,000.

That’s a different measure from the Census median owner value for Kirkwood (about $451,400), which reflects owners’ estimates of all homes, not recent sales.

Can I buy a house in Kirkwood for $400,000?

21 of 80 sales (26.2%) closed at $400,000 or less. Of those 21, 11 had three or more bedrooms, 10 had two or more baths and 11 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,263 sq ft, built in 1948.

What does $500,000 buy in Kirkwood?

35 of 80 sales (43.8%) closed at $500,000 or less. Of those 35, 23 had three or more bedrooms, 24 had two or more baths and 16 were ranch-style. Their median was 1,506.5 sq ft, built in 1948.

See what each budget reached →

How fast do houses sell in Kirkwood?

The median sale spent 6 days on market (CDOM). A quarter of the 80 sales took 3 days or less; the slowest quarter took about 22 days or more. 18 sales took 30+ days, 7 took 60+ and the longest took 157.

See market time by price band →

Do Kirkwood homes sell over asking?

Measured against the original list price, 45 of 80 (56.2%) closed above it, 7 (8.8%) at it and 28 (35.0%) below it. The median sale was 102.1% of the original ask.

See asking-price results by band →

What kind of houses are common in Kirkwood?

Mostly older houses. Of the 80 sales, 53 (66.2%) were built before 1960, 7 (8.8%) from 1960 to 1979, 7 (8.8%) from 1980 to 1999 and 13 (16.2%) in 2000 or later. The median house was about 2,315 sq ft, built in 1951, and 20 of 80 (25.0%) listed a ranch style.

See how the house changes by band →

Does a Kirkwood mailing address mean the house is in the City of Kirkwood?

Not necessarily. All 80 sales inside the city boundary were in ZIP 63122, but 63122 also covers neighboring communities. 66 of the 80 listed “Kirkwood” as the city in MLS and 14 listed “St Louis.” Check the St. Louis County GIS map for the exact address.

63122 ZIP code guide →

Is all of Kirkwood in the Kirkwood School District?

All 80 sales inside the city boundary this year were in the Kirkwood R-VII School District. District lines don’t always follow city limits, so verify the assigned schools for the exact address before you write an offer.

Schools and district boundaries →

Is Kirkwood its own city?

Yes. Kirkwood is an incorporated city in St. Louis County with its own city government. Before you close, ask the city what it requires when a house changes hands, such as an occupancy permit or inspection.

Methodology

How This Kirkwood Analysis Was Built

This analysis uses 80 closed-sale records from the STL Home Journey 2026 St. Louis County sales dataset (January 8–October 1, 2026). A sale counts here when its listing’s map coordinates fall inside the Census Bureau’s boundary for the City of Kirkwood, using the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2024 TIGER/Line place boundary. The same records feed the St. Louis County school district housing analysis.

  • Historical closed sales are not current inventory.
  • Days on market uses CDOM.
  • Sale ÷ original ask compares the close price with the original list price.
  • Ranch share counts any MLS style that includes “Ranch.”
  • Listing coordinates can be imprecise near the boundary. Verify jurisdiction and school assignment for the exact address.

The Honest Take

A Suburb With a Strong Center, Not Just Good Branding

Kirkwood stays on buyer shortlists because it offers more than name recognition. It has a real center of gravity: downtown, the train station, and a municipal identity that feels older and more established than a typical postwar suburb.

The Census Bureau lists Kirkwood's 2020 population at 29,461, and city materials describe Kirkwood as nine square miles in size. That is large enough to offer variety, but compact enough to still feel coherent.

When buyers say they want an inner-ring suburb with a real sense of place, Kirkwood is often what they are trying to describe.

Location & Identity

Downtown and the Train Station Still Set the Tone

The city's historic train station page says the Kirkwood station was built in 1893 and remains one of the city's most enduring buildings. That is not trivia; it helps explain why Kirkwood still feels anchored by place rather than by sheer subdivision spread.

The city also highlights Downtown Kirkwood as a distinct business district, which separates Kirkwood from suburbs that are mainly residential without a strong center.

Market Reality

What Buyers Actually Find Here

Kirkwood buyers are usually balancing charm, convenience, and price tolerance. It is a high-demand suburb, so buyers do not get Kirkwood value by accident.

There is meaningful variety inside the city — older homes near the center, more traditional suburban pockets, and different tradeoffs between lot size and proximity to downtown amenities.

What usually holds demand together is that the suburb feels complete. Buyers understand what they are getting, and that reduces hesitation.
